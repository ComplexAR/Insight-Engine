Triggers when the user asks to analyse a scenario or document, surface hidden or non-obvious issues, produce a defensible analysis or a decision brief, investigate what is really going on beneath a situation, or assess a high-stakes decision. Runs a fail-safe proportionality triage, an optional scoping step, a fixed provocation-page pass, web verification of load-bearing claims with source-tiering and a disconfirmation pass, a conditional systems-investigation pass (a graded causal map for feedback-driven problems; investigate, don't predict), deep-core routing, a forced deep-core judgement gate, an assumption-resilience check, and grade-locked assembly with a decision brief.

# Insight Engine — analyse

Run the Insight Engine on the problem. The large language model provides the insight; your job is to frame it, verify it, grade it, route what cannot be verified to the human, force a judgement on it, and deliver a defensible analysis plus a decision brief. Work the steps in order. Do not skip verification, and never change a grade once set (grades are SET at the Step 7 grade-lock; Step 3–4 provenance and evidence marks are provisional until then).

## Step 1 — Proportionality triage (run this FIRST — fail-safe; default to depth)
Before running the steps below, judge whether the problem is UNAMBIGUOUSLY low-stakes — routine, self-contained, reversible, with no third parties bound, no material money or dependency, no legal or regulatory exposure, and no contested values. Apply strong skepticism to the framing: ask "am I being invited to treat this as routine?" Treat ANY of the following as disqualifying — run the full method: irreversibility; anyone bound who is not in the room; material money or dependency; legal or regulatory exposure; contested values; a party who benefits from light scrutiny; anything not easily reversed. ONLY if the problem is unambiguously trivial on every count: give a proportionate short answer, skip the heavy machinery, say you have done so, and invite the operator to request the full pass. Under ANY doubt, run the full method — depth is the default and down-scoping is the rare exception. (Fail-safe by design, and validated this way: a mild over-analysis is cheap; wrongly down-scoping a problem that only looks routine is not.)

## Step 2 — Optional scoping (offer, never block)
Offer the operator up to five optional, skippable clarifying questions with AskUserQuestion — plain wording, one thing per question. Ask only what is not already clear. If they skip any or all, proceed on explicit stated assumptions and mark them as assumptions.
1. The trouble — what is going wrong or has to be decided, stated as the symptom, not a diagnosis (when in doubt, describe the trouble, not its cause) — and why now?
2. The owner — whose problem is this, who will act on the answer, and who else will read it (the intended audience)?
3. Success criteria and stakes — what would count as a good outcome, what counts as a serious cost, and whose costs matter most? (You articulate the value structure; the operator owns the value judgement.)
4. Constraints and scope — what is fixed or non-negotiable, and what is explicitly in and out of scope? (Scope is where the largest hiddens hide.)
5. Party-held material — documents or figures the operator holds that cannot be verified online; accept uploads.

If the operator skips scoping in full or in part, do not proceed silently: write a one-line problem statement — the trouble, the owner, and the core question — built from whatever answers WERE given, with the gaps filled by you and marked as assumptions; display it, then carry it into Step 3. (A skipped question never blocks; an unstated problem does.)

## Step 3 — The provocation pass (L0 + L1)
Load `references/provocation-page.md` and apply it verbatim. Treat your fluent first reading as the baseline a competent reader already has — not the finding. Run the two always-on moves (displacement / second-order; provenance `[V]`/`[N]` plus the single dominant unknown) and the candidate probes (absent voice; cui bono from the framing; base rate & measurement; normalised-as-natural; spuriously-present; conspicuously-absent / omission sweep; reflexive). Then steelman your strongest findings and name every materially affected party, including those not in the room.

## Step 4 — Verification (L2): verify before asserting
For every load-bearing claim, apply the three-bucket router:
- **Web-verifiable** (a public fact, pattern, or rule): search; grade `[V]` with a cited current source, or `[N]` if uncorroborated or contradicted. Correct your own confidently-wrong facts — a strong model will assert some, and this is where they get caught. Verify before relying; if the premise of the question is itself checkable, check it.
- **Party-held** (a private document or internal figure): do NOT pseudo-verify; record what document would settle it.
- **Irreducibly open** (a value, a framing, or a forecast): route to the human in Step 6.
Two disciplines make `[V]` mean something:
- **Tier every `[V]`** by source strength — `[V1]` primary (the regulation/statute, official statistic, peer-reviewed study, court ruling, primary dataset; primary means *proximate*, not trustworthy — an interested party's own primary material does not earn `[V1]` on a claim that party has a stake in), `[V2]` secondary (reputable reporting/interpretation resting on primaries), `[V3]` contested/weak (weak but *independent* corroboration, or credible sources disagree — a claim resting only on an interested party's own account is `[N]`, not `[V3]`). The tier is part of the grade.
- **Disconfirm the load-bearing few** — for each claim the call materially rests on, run a second search at its NEGATION; if credible counter-evidence of comparable strength exists, downgrade to `[V3]`/`[N]` and record the dispute; if a real negation search finds nothing, mark it *survived disconfirmation* — but only where the field holds at least two independent roots; if the corroboration traces to a single origin, mark it *unopposed — single-root record*, not survived.
- **The attribution ceiling** — "person X erred / failed / ignored" in any official account is an ATTRIBUTION, a recorded judgement by an interested party. That the body made the finding may grade up to `[V1]`; the blame itself is never graded as established fact and routes to the human with the deep core (Step 6).
Weight a self-damaging admission above a self-serving claim. Revise the dominant unknown against what you find. (The `verify` skill runs this step — router, tiering, and disconfirmation — standalone.)

## Step 5 — Systems-investigation pass (conditional; investigate, do not predict)
Run this only when the problem is genuinely systemic — when it shows **two or more** of: feedback / circular causation; accumulation (stocks that build up or drain); material delays between cause and effect; multiple interacting actors; a possible regime shift / tipping; self-fulfilling expectations. Otherwise skip it with one line — *"no material feedback structure — systems pass not warranted"* — and go to Step 6. When it runs, record which of the signs fired (a one-line telemetry note), so trigger calibration can be reviewed from real use. (Proportionate by design: do not force a causal map onto a static or one-shot problem; importance, emotion, or difficulty is not systemic structure, and a spurious map is a failure, not thoroughness.)

If it runs, build a **causal map as a graded hypothesis — not a simulation and not a forecast.** Keep it to the load-bearing few: ≤8 variables, ≤5 loops, ≤4 tipping points, ≤5 leverage points (a sprawling map is itself false precision).
- **Variables & links.** Name the key variables (mark accumulators as stocks). Write each causal link `A --(+/−[, delay])--> B` with a grade and a one-line mechanism. Grade links `[V1]` established mechanism / `[V2]` reputable consensus / `[V3]` disputed or weak / `[N]` your structural inference. A `[V1]`/`[V2]` grade requires cited in-case or in-domain support for the mechanism *as it operates here*; a mechanism carried over by analogy from another domain, without in-case support, caps at `[V3]`. The grade attests the mechanism exists in general — not that this arrow is the operative, correctly-directed, un-confounded cause in this case. Flag material delays — a lag is where the system feels fine until it isn't. Do not assert magnitudes beyond what is `[V1]`-grounded.
- **Feedback loops.** Identify reinforcing (R) and balancing (B) loops. Grade each **twice** — *exists* (the chain is real) and *dominates-now* (it currently drives behaviour). Existence may be `[V]`; **dominance is usually `[N]` — never assert which loop wins as fact; route it to the human.** Before finalising, scan the open causal chains for feedback that closes but was not drawn (models systematically under-supply loops), and re-check polarity on any decreasing-language link ("the less X, the less Y" — a known sign-flip).
- **Tipping points.** Name candidate regime-shift thresholds **as conditions, never dates or probabilities**, each with reversibility (reversible / hard / irreversible) and a grade (`[N]` unless a specific threshold is evidenced). Name them to *watch*, not to predict.
- **Emergence.** Name any behaviour that arises from the interaction and is in no single part — especially self-fulfilling expectations, where the shared narrative is itself a causal variable.
- **Leverage points (ranked).** Where would a small intervention move the system most — the dominant loop's entry node, a delay, or the driving expectations? Rank them; grade each **twice** — *mechanism* (why structurally high-leverage) and *effectiveness* (whether acting there will actually work — usually `[N]`).
- **The missing driver (omission check).** Maps most often err by leaving a variable OUT, not by mis-drawing one that is in. End with one line naming the most plausible driver **not** in this map that would change the call if it were real — the counter to omission, which no check on the drawn arrows can catch.
- **Boundary disclosure.** State in one line whose problem-framing this map adopts, who is materially affected but absent from the sources it was built from, and what was placed outside its boundary — flagged as a *value judgement, distinct from `[N]` evidential uncertainty*. If a materially-affected party is absent, add one line on how the map would differ under their framing and carry it to the dominant unknown / what-to-verify-first.

**The hard ceiling (the spine of this pass):** structure may be graded up to `[V]`; **whether or when the system tips has a ceiling of `[N]` and routes to the human.** State the conditions under which it could shift; never that it will, or when, or with what probability. The map is a hypothesis to be falsified, not a model to be trusted. The map — its variables, links, grades, and the `[N]` ceiling on dominance and tipping — is part of the grade-locked spine: a downstream `render` may re-voice it for an audience but must not strip the grades, present a loop as dominant, or turn a condition into a prediction (this governs any diagram or causal-loop-diagram rendering). This pass investigates structure; it does NOT simulate dynamics or compute outcomes — if genuine quantitative modelling is needed, say so and route to specialists and data.

Feed forward: loop *dominance*, *regime*, and *timing* join the deep-core OPEN list (Step 6 → the gate); the ranked **leverage points** inform the call (Step 9, brief item a); the **tipping conditions** inform what-to-verify-first / monitor (9d) and what-would-flip-the-call (9e); and the Step 7 resilience check stress-tests the call against the map's load-bearing links and loops.

## Step 6 — Deep-core routing
Separate the material issues that are NOT externally verifiable — the framing of the decision itself; legitimacy and who is bound without a say; opportunity cost and unmodelled alternatives; value trade-offs — into an explicit OPEN list. Do not resolve them, do not drop them, and do not assert them as verified. These are the questions the operator must judge in Step 8.

## Step 7 — Grade-lock, then resilience-check (the invariants)
Fix every claim's `[V]`/`[N]` grade with its source tier (`[V1]`/`[V2]`/`[V3]`), the single dominant residual unknown, and the non-droppable caveat core (all grades + the dominant unknown + a one-line reliability caveat naming what the analysis rests on, e.g. a single interested source or a `[V3]` the call leans on). Nothing downstream may change a grade — only re-express it. The call, its flip conditions, and its action contingency (and, once introduced, any strength label) travel as part of this locked spine: a downstream reader-facing `render` may re-voice them for an audience but must not soften or drop them — it must not turn a firm call into "we suggest", or quietly remove a flip condition.
Then **stress-test the conclusion, not just the claims.** Name the two to four assumptions the call most rests on, vary them *together*, and record whether the conclusion survives. This is an **assumption-fragility** check: it exposes where jointly-moved assumptions would break the call. It is NOT system modelling — it does not simulate feedback loops, cascades, or tipping points, and must not claim to (the conditional Step 5 systems-investigation pass is where graded structure is mapped). Feed the result into the confidence basis and what-would-flip-it in Step 9. State the coverage plainly: which assumptions you varied together, and which load-bearing ones you did not — do not imply a fraction of the assumption space was covered (that denominator is unknowable).

## Step 8 — The deep-core judgement GATE (forced; the operator clears it)
Before you write the final brief, **stop and put the deep-core open questions to the operator as a gate.** Put the framing / legitimacy / opportunity-cost / value questions from Step 6 to the operator **one at a time** with AskUserQuestion — never as a wall of questions. Word each concretely and plainly (no analyst shorthand), and include a one-line example answer so the operator can see what a position looks like. Alongside the substantive options, always offer **"I don't know — explain this"** (explain the question in plain language with one concrete example of what turns on it, then ask again) and **defer** (a deferral is logged, not ignored). Keep the gate to a handful of exchanges: where Step 5 fed dominance / regime / timing items into the OPEN list, fold them into a single systems question rather than one each. Ask for a one-line position on each. **Do not finalise the brief until the operator has taken a position on each or chosen to defer.**

One-liners frame the gate — quick free-text lines, not judgement questions (no example-answer or explain machinery needed; explain on request). **Open** it by asking the operator to record, in one line, their own standpoint or interest in the matter — the analysis has audited the problem; this line audits the map-maker — before the framing and legitimacy questions. Only if the Step 5 systems pass ran, **add** one question: "at what level do we intervene — this problem, or the problem it is a symptom of?"

Why this is a gate, not a heading: controlled testing showed that merely leading the brief with these questions — even calling them mandatory — does NOT get a decision-maker to act on them; they read them, then prioritise the verifiable. The deep core is a **judgement the human takes**, not a priority that competes with "commission a study." Forcing an explicit position is what actually gets it addressed. Keep it light — a handful of questions, one position each, deferral always available. You articulate the questions and the value structure; the operator owns the judgement.

## Step 9 — Assemble, with the operator's judgements and the decision brief (L4)
First, a **coverage-retention check**: having concentrated on the load-bearing few (Step 4) and on the dominant unknown and deep core (Steps 7-8), re-scan once for material issues that this focus deprioritised, and fold any that resurface back into the findings. Sharpening the spotlight darkens its edges; this is the counter-sweep.

Then produce the deliverable in this order:
1. **The deep-core questions with the operator's recorded positions** (or noted deferrals) — leading the document. This is the gate, cleared and on the record.
2. **The verified findings** — each hidden with its `[V]`/`[N]` grade, source tier, and any disconfirmation result, plus its source, stated so the reader can validate it without help (web-verified evidence: the named source with its URL; party-held or in-document evidence: the document name plus section or page). Separate documented patterns from any interest- or grief-driven over-claims, and say which you are setting aside and why. Where the verified evidence shows the outcome is being determined by power rather than merits — a party that can simply impose its preference — say so plainly ("this is a power problem, not an analysis problem") and downgrade any option premised on accommodation the powerful party has no need to grant. Carry any official attribution of individual or group fault as the issuing body's recorded finding — never in your own voice as settled fact. For any finding adverse to a named party, record whether that party was given the opportunity to respond and whether they contested it (**responded / contested / not offered the opportunity**) — "contested by the party it damages" is load-bearing and belongs on the finding.
3. **The decision brief — always include this block:** (a) **the call** — the actual recommendation. *If the problem is wicked* (the deep core dominates and there is no verifiable solution), the call is a **provisional stance, not a verdict**: state which value it trades against which ("this prioritises X at the expense of Y"), mark it explicitly re-openable, name what would re-open it, and offer to hand off to `track` — emitting the re-openers and the (e) flip conditions as a discrete trigger list `track` can watch (each: indicator -> what it would change). A crisp verdict on a wicked problem re-imports the false closure the analysis exists to avoid. (b) **confidence basis** — your confidence in the fact-chain and the conditions under which it changes, naming the weakest load-bearing grade (any `[V3]` or `[N]` the call leans on). This is evidence-quality transparency, not a track-record calibration — do not imply a scored probability. Then state, categorically and separately, the **action contingency**: what must hold true *after* acting for the call to succeed (an intervention can fail even when every premise is `[V1]`); this is distinct from what would flip the evidence in (e). (c) **the dominant unknown** — the one fact that would most move it. (d) **what to verify first** — the cheapest next step that most reduces risk. (e) **what would flip the call** — including any jointly-moved assumptions the Step-7 resilience check found the call could not survive, and any tipping conditions from the Step 5 systems pass. Phrase each as a first-person conditional ("I am treating X as true; if X is false, the call reverses"). **This call rests on the operator's recorded gate positions** (Step 8): name the position(s) it depends on and state that revising them voids the call; if the operator skipped the gate, say so and name the default assumption the call embeds in its place. **Hedge discipline:** every hedge in the brief — the provisional-stance marker, the confidence caveat, any "conditional" — must bind to a named grade, flip condition, gate position, or re-opener, and the stacked hedges must reference the *same* named conditions rather than adding separate qualifiers; a hedge that names nothing is banned. **Keep the brief a short decision surface:** each element earns its line, and a new consideration merges into an existing element rather than becoming another field.
4. **Sources.**
5. **Plain-language legend** — close every full-pass deliverable with this fixed block, verbatim (a triage-down-scoped short answer carries no legend): *How to read this: [V] = independently corroborated; [N] = not independently verified — it rests on an interested or self-reporting party with nothing independent behind it, or could not be checked. Tiers on a [V]: [V1] confirmed against a primary source (the regulation, ruling, official statistic, or study itself) — primary means proximate, not trustworthy, so an interested party's own document does not by itself earn [V1] on a claim it has a stake in; [V2] confirmed against reputable secondary reporting; [V3] weakly but independently corroborated, or credible sources disagree. "Survived disconfirmation" = a genuine search for counter-evidence found nothing credible and the corroboration spans at least two independent sources; a single-root record is marked "unopposed", not "survived". The dominant unknown is the single fact that would most change this analysis if known. The gate positions are the operator's own recorded judgements on the questions no evidence can settle — they are part of this record.*
Do not over-claim causation; an over-claim costs more than a miss. Hold the reliability caveat to the end.

## Step 10 — Offer independent adjudication (conditional, OPT-IN, off by default)
After the brief is assembled, consider offering an independent second pass — the `adjudicate` skill (Step 10) — but only when warranted, and **never automatically**. Run the necessity gate and **declare the result either way** ("adjudication not warranted here because ..." / "warranted because ..."):
- only if the proportionality triage rated this **high-stakes or wicked**;
- only if a **live, contestable judgment remains** (contested attribution, which loop dominates in the systems map, a value/framing call, or a load-bearing claim graded weak or uncorroborated) — if the disciplined pass already settled everything, adjudication is redundant;
- for privileged or confidential matters, the cross-lab rung is **blocked by default** (external egress); it is operator-overridable by a deliberate typed act and logged, while the in-boundary rungs (Fable / Opus panel / self-adversarial) remain available.

If the operator has set a **standing opt-out**, do not raise it — this is checked by code, not memory: run `python prefs/prefs.py should-offer --rung <A|B|C>` in the `adjudicate` skill folder (exit 0 = offer, 1 = suppressed at that scope). Otherwise **offer** it — a real, one-word-declinable choice ("run an independent check on this, or skip?") — on this and every future qualifying run, until they opt out. It is **opt-in and off by default**: every rung (a different-lab model, Fable 5, an Opus panel, or a self-adversarial reset) costs credits and/or setup, so nothing runs without an explicit yes. On acceptance, hand off to the `adjudicate` skill, which runs a blind-then-adversarial pass down the independence ladder, folds any discrepancies back under grade-lock — **it never flips your call** — declares which rung it reached, and logs the run to the real-use monitor. A decline is respected: no caveat in the deliverable, no re-asking within this analysis. **Honest framing:** present it as a rigour-and-defensibility check that sharpens a sound analysis; its decision-flip benefit is unproven and it is not a safety net.

## Step 11 — Offer render
Offer to render the analysis for specific audiences (the `render` skill) — re-voicing it for a board, a regulator, opposing counsel, or a lay reader without changing any claim or grade.

## Step 12 — Offer track
For a long-running inquiry, offer to open or update a living workspace (the `track` skill) — keeping the grade-locked spine current and re-verifying as new facts arrive.
