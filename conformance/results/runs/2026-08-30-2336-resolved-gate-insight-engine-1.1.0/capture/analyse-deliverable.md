### 2.3 Findings

**Find1 — One of the three options may not exist as described.** The BSF closed to new applications on 1 September 2025 and CSS is now the single route for buildings over 11 metres (ClaimReg1, [V2]). "Waiting for the outcome of our Building Safety Fund application" is a real option only if that application pre-dates 1 September 2025 or has already been transferred. If it is an old BSF *social-sector* claim, it was scoped to the value of work that would otherwise have been charged to leaseholders (ClaimReg3, [V1]) — a small share of a mostly-social block — whereas a CSS application under the post-11-June-2025 rules covers social rented units on the same basis as leasehold (ClaimReg2, [V1]). Option 2 may therefore be waiting for a materially smaller sum, through a route that has been superseded, when a larger award is available on a different route. Which of these is true is PartyHeld4.

**Find2 — The waking watch is unrecoverable under every option, and no option addresses it.** CSS will not fund waking watch running costs (ClaimReg4, [V1]). The £310,000 a year is therefore a pure loss in all three scenarios, and its total size is set by how long the building stays unremediated — that is, by how long the decision takes — not by which option is chosen. It is also the only cost in the case that a well-understood substitution can attack: MHCLG research found alarm installation pays back against a waking watch within three to six months (ClaimReg12, [V2]), and £417 per flat per month sits near the top of the published range (ClaimReg13, [U]). None of the three options touches it. This is the largest omission in the option set.

**Find3 — Option 1 as framed may forfeit grant that is currently available.** CSS tells applicants not to apply if they do not intend to use taxpayer funding, and funds social housing works commencing after 11 June 2025 (ClaimReg17, [V1]). Whether committing £4.8m of the association's own reserves now forecloses a CSS award — and whether signing a works contract outside CSS puts the building into the "remediated outside CSS, not eligible for new funding" category (ClaimReg2, [V1]) — is the question the board has not been asked. It costs one call to Homes England to answer.

**Find4 — The unestablished overcladding date is the hinge, not a housekeeping gap.** If the overcladding was completed within the 30 years before 28 June 2022, a Defective Premises Act claim against whoever commissioned and carried it out may be live (ClaimReg6, [V2]) — and CSS requires the association to have taken all reasonable steps to pursue exactly that, monitors progress on it, and requires repayment of any damages recovered (ClaimReg5, [V1]). If it pre-dates that window, no such claim exists and the association is the payer of last resort. The board is being asked to allocate £4.8m without knowing which of these worlds it is in. Note that the question of recovery is separate from the question of charging leaseholders: paragraph 8 of Schedule 8 blocks charging qualifying leaseholders for cladding remediation regardless of when the cladding was installed (ClaimReg7, [V2]).

**Find5 — The answer to the two leaseholders is largely fixed by law, not by board discretion.** For qualifying leases, no service charge is payable in respect of cladding remediation (ClaimReg7, [V2]). Whether each of these leases qualifies is PartyHeld5. The exposure here is not the money; it is the silence. They have written, and a non-answer is itself a decision that the association will have to defend.

**Find6 — Option 3 is not a cheaper version of option 1; it is a different legal position.** PAS 9980 does support proportionate mitigation, and CSS funds according to what the FRAEW recommends (ClaimReg16, [V1]). So a £1.6m cavity-barrier scheme is defensible if, and only if, the FRAEW says the residual risk is tolerable once the barriers are made good. If the FRAEW recommends removing the insulation, then £1.6m buys a scheme that does not discharge the section 84 duty (ClaimReg9, [V2]), does not end the waking watch, and — as works not recommended in the FRAEW — is at risk of not being grant-eligible (ClaimReg16 and ClaimReg17, both [V1]). The board is being offered a middle option whose entire defensibility rests on a document that has not been quoted to it.

**Find7 — Telling residents nothing is a live compliance exposure, not only poor practice.** The RSH expects earliest-opportunity notification of material issues (ClaimReg14, [V2]); the CSS guidance carries a dedicated section on keeping leaseholders and residents informed (ClaimReg2 source document, [V1]); and higher-risk building duties include engaging residents (ClaimReg9, [V2]). Three further weeks of silence while a board deliberates is the most reversible bad outcome in this case and the cheapest to fix.

**Find8 — On power: the association does not control this timetable, and options premised on the idea that it does should be downgraded.** No counterparty can simply impose its preference, so this is not a pure power problem. But Homes England sets the grant rules, the RSH and the Building Safety Regulator can compel, and the fire and rescue service can serve a prohibition or enforcement notice — a route the CSS guidance itself says government supports and that Homes England and MHCLG will help consider where projects do not progress ([V1], CSS overview, "Accelerating remediation and enforcement"). Option 2 in particular assumes the association keeps the choice while it waits. It may not.

**Find9 — Displacement.** The reading that option 3 "saves £3.2m" displaces cost onto three unmeasured places: onto residents, who keep the residual risk and the waking watch; onto the future, in the form of a second scheme at a later, higher price; and onto the grant position, if the works fall outside what the FRAEW recommends. A saving that appears only because the destination of the cost is unmeasured is not a saving.

**Find10 — What the framing does.** Three precise numbers presented as a decision surface make this look like an allocation problem. The only document that determines which of the three is even lawful — the FRAEW — is not quoted. A three-option paper with a moderate middle option also makes £1.6m look like prudence rather than like a legal position that has not been tested. Whoever prepared it may be under real pressure to protect the development programme; that pressure is legitimate, but it should be visible to the board rather than embedded in the option set.

**Find11 — Absent voices.** Materially affected and not in the room: the 62 households; the households waiting for the 90 homes; future purchasers and their mortgage lenders, whose willingness to lend sets whether leasehold flats here can be sold at all; the waking watch staff; and whoever commissioned the overcladding, who may be liable and has not been put on notice.

**Find12 — Base rate.** Across the sector, external wall remediation costs have systematically exceeded first estimates and programmes have run late. Treat £4.8m as a floor, not a price, until PartyHeld3 says otherwise.

**Set aside, and why:** any reading that treats the two leaseholders' letters as the main legal risk. They are the visible correspondence, but 53-or-so social tenants carry the same physical risk with fewer routes to complain, and the association's larger exposure is regulatory rather than contractual.

## 4. Assumption-resilience check

Assumptions the call most rests on, varied together:

- **Assump1** — the FRAEW recommends works beyond cavity barriers, so a partial scheme would leave residual risk requiring the waking watch to continue.
- **Assump2** — the live application is a pre-September-2025 BSF social-sector claim scoped to leaseholder-attributable costs, or otherwise not a full-scope CSS application.
- **Assump3** — the overcladding post-dates June 1992, so a recovery route exists.
- **Assump4** — the development programme can absorb delay without breaching a covenant or a grant condition.

**Result.** Moving Assump3 and Assump4 together does not break the call: losing the recovery route removes an upside rather than the necessity, and a covenant problem changes the financing route rather than the priority. Moving Assump1 and Assump2 together **does** break it: if the FRAEW supports a partial scheme *and* the application is already a full-scope CSS one under the new rules, then waiting is rational and option 3 is defensible, and the recommendation below reverses.

**Coverage, stated plainly.** I varied those four together. Load-bearing assumptions I did not vary: that £4.8m and £1.6m are of the right order; that no enforcement notice is currently in force; and that this is the association's only affected block. I am not claiming a fraction of the assumption space was covered — that denominator is unknowable.

## 5. The decision brief

### (a) The call

**Recommend that the board adopt none of the three options as framed, and instead take a decision in three weeks on a corrected option set.** The three options rest on a document the board has not seen and a funding route that may have been superseded; choosing between them now is choosing in the dark, and two of the three carry irreversible consequences (TipCond2, TipCond3).

Put to the board, in the paper going out now, a two-week evidence sprint with five items, each of which is cheap and none of which forecloses anything:

1. Quote the FRAEW's risk rating and its recommended works verbatim in the board paper (LevPoint4).
2. Establish which scheme the live application sits in, and if it is not a CSS application under the post-11-June-2025 social-sector rules, apply or transfer (LevPoint2).
3. Establish the overcladding date and the commissioning party from building control, planning and contract records, and put any identifiable party on notice (LevPoint1).
4. Obtain a costed common-alarm proposal to end the waking watch, and confirm whether the Waking Watch Replacement Fund is still open to this building (LevPoint3).
5. Write to all 62 households, and answer the two leaseholders in terms consistent with ClaimReg7 (LevPoint5).

Then the board decides between full remediation funded primarily through CSS with reserves used as a bridge, and a partial scheme **only if the FRAEW supports it in terms**.

**On the allocation question, this is a provisional stance and not a verdict**, because the deep core dominates it and the operator has deferred GateQ1. The stance: if the FRAEW recommends removal of the insulation, the block takes priority over the development programme's *timing*. **This prioritises the physical safety of 62 households already living with the risk at the expense of the delivery date for 90 households not yet housed** — a real cost to real people, borne by people who were not asked. It is explicitly re-openable. What re-opens it: the board answering GateQ1 differently; the FRAEW supporting a partial scheme; or the discovery that this is not the association's only affected block.

**Confidence in the fact-chain.** Strong on the funding rules: the Cladding Safety Scheme guidance was opened and identity-checked, and ClaimReg2, ClaimReg4, ClaimReg5, ClaimReg16 and ClaimReg17 are [V1] quotations from it. Any of these can fail even if every premise above is [V1].

### (c) The dominant unknown

**What the PAS 9980 FRAEW actually recommends.** It determines which of the three options is lawful, which is grant-eligible, whether the waking watch can end, and whether the section 84 duty is discharged. It exists, the association commissioned it, and it has not been quoted to the board. Nothing else in this case would move the analysis as much.

### (d) What to verify first

Read the FRAEW's section 15 summary and its recommendations and quote them. Cost: a few hours of one person's time, no money, no commitment. Then one telephone call to Homes England to establish the scheme identity of the live application and whether starting works prejudices an award. Those two steps between them resolve the dominant unknown and Assump2, which is the pair the call cannot survive.

### (e) What would flip the call

- I am treating the FRAEW as recommending works beyond cavity barriers. **If it rates the wall tolerable once the barriers are made good, option 3 becomes the defensible call** and the waking watch can probably end.
- I am treating the live application as either superseded or scoped only to leaseholder-attributable costs. **If it is a full-scope live CSS application already in assessment, waiting becomes rational** and the call moves toward option 2.
- Jointly: **if both of the above are false at once, this call does not survive** — that is the Assump1-plus-Assump2 pair from the resilience check.

- I am treating this as the association's only block with an external wall problem. **If it is not, the decision belongs at portfolio level** and the reserves arithmetic here is wrong.
- I am treating the £4.8m as an estimate of the right order. **If it is a tendered price that has already moved, or a floor that will move a long way, the reserves headroom argument changes.**

## 7. Plain-language legend

*How to read this: [V] = verified, independently corroborated; [U] = unverified, meaning not independently verified. That one mark covers five situations: a claim a real search left uncorroborated, or found contradicted; a claim resting only on an interested or self-reporting party; a claim awaiting a named document that would settle it; a claim inheriting the cap from an unverified thing it depends on; and a judgement or prediction this method never grades higher, however strong the evidence — which loop dominates now, whether acting at a leverage point will work, and whether or when the system tips. The first three can move if someone looks harder. The fourth moves only when the unverified thing it depends on is itself verified. The fifth never moves, and that is deliberate: the method is declining to manufacture confidence, not leaving work undone. The claim's own line says which of the five it is, and records any contradiction found. Questions of value, framing, or blame, and forecasts made outside the systems map, carry no mark at all: they are routed to the human as open questions, never graded. Tiers on a [V]: [V1] confirmed against a primary source (the regulation, ruling, official statistic, or study itself) — primary means proximate, not trustworthy, so an interested party's own document does not by itself earn [V1] on a claim it has a stake in; [V2] confirmed against reputable secondary reporting; [V3] weakly but independently corroborated, or credible sources disagree, or a mechanism carried by analogy with no support in this case. Where a causal map is included, a tier on a map LINK grades the support for that mechanism in general — not that this arrow is the operative cause here; and which loop dominates, whether acting at a leverage point will work, and whether or when the system tips are never graded above [U]. "Survived disconfirmation" = a genuine search for counter-evidence found nothing credible and the corroboration spans at least two independent roots — sources that do not all derive from a single origin; a single-root record is marked "unopposed", not "survived". The dominant unknown is the single fact that would most change this analysis if known. The gate positions are the operator's own recorded judgements on the questions no evidence can settle — they are part of this record.*
