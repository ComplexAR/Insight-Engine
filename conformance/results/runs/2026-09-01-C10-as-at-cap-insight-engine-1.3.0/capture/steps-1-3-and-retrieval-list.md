## Step 3 — provocation pass

> **Displacement.** The word doing the most work in your question is "minimum." A minimum is a floor,
> and the cost of treating it as the answer lands on the employer: if the contract gives more, paying
> the statutory figure is a breach of contract, and it is a breach the employee can prove from one
> document. The second displacement is the date. "Next month" is a fixed endpoint attached to a
> process whose outcome is not yet decided.
>
> **Cui bono from the framing.** Posed this way, a fairness problem becomes an arithmetic problem
> with two boxes. A manager who receives "eight weeks" and "yes, they qualify" has both boxes ticked
> and still has not been told the thing that decides the case, which is whether the process was
> reasonable.
>
> **Spuriously present.** "Eight years" is precise, and nearly all of its precision is idle. It
> clears the qualifying threshold with years to spare, so on question (b) it is not close. Its only
> real arithmetic use is converting to notice weeks.
>
> **Normalised as natural.** That the two questions are independent. They are not. Giving correct
> notice does not cure an unfair process, and paying more than the minimum does not either.
>
> **Absent voice.** The employee, obviously. Less obviously: whoever will hear an appeal.
>
> **Omission sweep** — twelve items, of which the first is what the conduct actually is, "missing
> from a question that asserts conduct as the reason".
>
> **Reflexive.** You asked for something short, for a line manager, with "two things" that need to be
> right. That is pressure toward exactly the two-number answer, and I am flagging it rather than
> complying with it silently.
>
> **Steelman.** My strongest finding is that the timetable is the risk. The best opposing reading: a
> month is not short. […] That reading is good, and it forces the finding into a narrower and better
> form — the exposure is not haste, it is that a termination date appears settled before the hearing
> that is supposed to decide it. Predetermination, not speed.
>
> **Provisional dominant unknown.** Not the notice figure. It is what evidence establishes conduct as
> the operative reason for dismissal, as distinct from the stated reason.

**Step 5 decision:** systems pass not warranted. *"Of the six signs, at most one fires weakly
(multiple interacting actors). A causal map here would be false precision."*
