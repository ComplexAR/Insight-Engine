# Which day centre to close — analysis and decision brief

**Problem statement.** A charity faces a cost-driven decision about two day centres; the eastern centre serves more people, the western centre serves people with no alternative within twenty miles; the trustee board wants a recommendation on Friday. The owner is the trustee board. The stated tie-breaker is the financial survival of the charity. Closing one centre is **not** already settled (operator's own scoping answer). No figures, documents, or jurisdiction were supplied.

**Assumptions carried, and marked as assumptions.** (a) The charity is in England, Wales or Scotland — inferred from "charity", "trustee board" and "day centre"; not stated by the operator, and the legal findings below are jurisdiction-dependent. (b) "Serves more people" means headcount, not attendance-days or intensity of need. (c) The board has authority to decide without a third party's consent. None of these was verified.

### Disconfirmation records

- **Disconf1 — against ClaimReg5** (closure harms attenders). A negation search for closures with neutral or positive outcomes returned one instance of successful transfer (Derbyshire, two users) against multiple documented failures of alternative provision. The harm finding **survived disconfirmation** — the corroboration spans at least two independent roots (US peer-reviewed cohort research and UK sector and press reporting).
- **Disconf2 — against ClaimReg8** (site closure does not release full site cost). A negation search did not return direct sector evidence that single-site closures deliver their modelled saving, and did not return direct evidence that they do not. The accounting treatment is corroborated; the applied claim is **unopposed — single-root record**, not survived, and is graded accordingly.
- **Disconf3 — against ClaimReg4** (judicial review risk). The negation search returned a counter-case: the High Court dismissed a renewed permission application over day care centre closures, and at least one council successfully defended a closure challenge. The risk is therefore real but not uniform, which is why ClaimReg4 sits at [V2] and is stated as exposure rather than as outcome.

### Findings

**Find1 — the question as posed cannot be answered on the information given, and this is the load-bearing finding.** The board has been handed one fact about breadth of need (eastern: more people) and one about depth of need (western: no alternative within twenty miles), and has adopted a criterion — the charity's financial survival — on which neither fact bears. Under that criterion the decisive quantities are avoidable cost, attributable income, and closure liabilities per site. None was supplied [ClaimReg10, ClaimReg1].

**Find2 — the binary is not established, and the operator has said so.** The board is being asked to choose between two options inside a frame that the operator's own scoping answer states is unsettled. Documented alternatives short of a whole-site closure exist [ClaimReg6]. A framing that presents two named losers removes from view the options that would cost someone in the room something: reduced opening days at both sites, cuts to central cost, hibernation of one site rather than disposal, asset transfer to a community body, or merger. *Cui bono* from the binary: whoever's budget line is not on the table.

**Find3 — the saving is probably smaller than the site's cost line.** Central costs are apportioned, not attached [ClaimReg8]; lease, dilapidation and redundancy are cash costs incurred at the moment of closure, and against a stated criterion of *survival*, near-term cash matters more than annualised saving. A closure can therefore worsen the position in year one while improving it in year three. Whether that is true here is [U] and party-held [ClaimReg10].

**Find4 — displacement: neither closure removes the cost, and the stated criterion is blind to where it lands.** Closing the western site moves cost onto unpaid family carers and, on the documented pattern, onto health and crisis services [ClaimReg5, ClaimReg7]. Closing the eastern site moves a larger volume of cost onto people who, by the framing's own admission, have alternatives. Because the adopted criterion is the charity's own solvency, both displacements are off the charity's books and invisible to the test being applied. That is not an argument against the criterion; it is a statement of what the criterion does not see, which the board should record that it saw.

**Find5 — "serves more people" is a proxy, and a gameable one.** Headcount, attendance-days, and intensity of need are three different measures that routinely rank the same two sites differently. Nothing in the material says which was used. A board minute resting on "more people" without naming the measure is a board minute that cannot be defended later.

**Find6 — the western closure carries the survival risk, which is the operator's own criterion.** If the charity delivers either centre under a commissioned contract, s.149(2) Equality Act 2010 can bind it in respect of that function [ClaimReg3], and closure decisions affecting people with no alternative are the ones that have attracted successful legal challenge [ClaimReg4] — though not uniformly [Disconf3]. Concentrated, un-substitutable loss is also what generates campaigns, press coverage, and commissioner rupture [ClaimReg9]. On a criterion of financial survival, this exposure is a financial fact, not a sentimental one.

**Find7 — this may be a power problem rather than an analysis problem, and the map does not yet show who holds the power.** If a local authority or NHS body commissions either service, that body — not the board — may effectively determine the outcome, and it may have its own duties triggered by the loss of the only provider in a catchment [ClaimReg12, ClaimReg3]. If so, the charity's real move is not to choose a site but to put the western closure to the commissioner as a live prospect and see whether money appears. Any option premised on the commissioner voluntarily accommodating the charity, absent that pressure, should be downgraded: a funder under no obligation to grant an accommodation generally does not.

**Find8 — the Friday deadline is itself a finding.** An irreversible decision, taken in five days, without contribution data, by a body carrying a legal duty to take reasonable steps [ClaimReg2], is defensible only if the cash position forces the timetable. If it does not, the deadline is a self-imposed constraint being treated as an external one — normalised as natural through repetition of the board calendar.

**Attribution ceiling.** No finding here attributes fault to any named person or body. No party adverse to a finding was offered the opportunity to respond, because no party was identified — **not offered the opportunity** applies across the register.

**Materially affected parties, including those not in the room.** Attenders at both centres; family and unpaid carers; centre staff and volunteers; any commissioning authority; other local providers who would absorb displaced demand; future attenders in the western catchment who are not yet users and cannot be consulted; donors and funders; the trustees personally, in respect of their duties. **None of them supplied any material for this analysis.**

## 4. Assumption-resilience check

Assumptions the call most rests on:

- **Assump1** — the deficit is structural, not a timing or cashflow problem.
- **Assump2** — a whole-site closure is the cheapest available way to close the gap.
- **Assump3** — income does not follow attendance so closely that closing the larger site loses more than it saves.
- **Assump4** — no restricted fund, lease term or contract clause pre-determines the outcome.

**Varied jointly.** Assump2 × Assump4 moved together against the call: if the eastern lease has no break and back-office reduction could close a material share of the gap, then neither closure is the cheapest route and the call **strengthens**. Assump1 × Assump3 moved together against the call: if the deficit is a timing problem and income follows attendance, closing either site is actively harmful and the call **strengthens**. Assump1 × Assump2 moved the other way: if the deficit is structural, large, and reserves are exhausted within weeks, a decision on incomplete data is forced and the call **weakens** — to "decide on Friday, and minute that you decided without contribution data, and why."

**Coverage, stated plainly.** I varied Assump1×Assump3 and Assump2×Assump4 jointly, and Assump1×Assump2 in the adverse direction. I did **not** vary two load-bearing assumptions: that the two centres are substitutable for one another's users, and that the board can decide without a commissioner's consent. No fraction of the assumption space is claimed; that denominator is unknowable.

## 5. Decision brief

**(a) The call — a provisional stance, explicitly re-openable, not a verdict.**

This is a wicked problem: the deep core dominates, and no amount of verification settles the trade between many people losing a replaceable service and few losing an irreplaceable one.

**Do not name a centre on Friday. Put a different, decidable motion to the board instead:** (i) approve a two-week contribution analysis — attributable income and avoidable cost per site, closure liabilities per site, restricted-fund and lease position; (ii) impose an immediate freeze on discretionary and central spend, which is the only cut that does not fire the decline loops; (iii) open the position formally with any commissioner before, not after, a site is named; (iv) name the decision date the data supports.

**If the board will not move the date, then the western centre is the wrong one to close first — on the board's own stated criterion.** Not because concentrated harm is worse, which is the value question the gate did not reach, but because concentrated, un-substitutable loss is where legal challenge, commissioner rupture and campaign risk concentrate [Find6, ClaimReg3, ClaimReg4, ClaimReg9], and on a criterion of *financial survival* that exposure is a financial fact. This second limb is weaker than the first and rests on [V2] and [U] material.

**This prioritises the charity's institutional survival and its legal defensibility at the expense of speed, and at the expense of the eastern attenders whose service this ranking places closer to the exit.** That is the trade being made, and it is the operator's stated criterion doing the work, not mine.

**Re-openers and flip conditions, as a trigger list for `track`:**

| Indicator | What it would change |
|---|---|
| Reserves fall below closure execution cost (TipCond1) | The two-week analysis becomes unaffordable; the call collapses to an insolvency-process decision |
| Commissioner signals re-tender (TipCond2) | Removes the leverage in limb (iii); the western position becomes indefensible |
| Attendance at either site drops below staffing viability (TipCond3) | The board's choice is overtaken; the site closes itself |
| Contribution analysis shows the eastern site is the loss-maker | Limb two reverses on its own criterion |
| Any restricted fund or lease tied to one site emerges | The missing driver is real; the whole map is re-derived |
| Operator answers any gate question | The call is voided and re-derived — the defaults below are doing real work |

**(b) Confidence basis and action contingency.**

Confidence in the fact-chain is **low on the case and moderate on the domain**. The weakest load-bearing grades the call leans on: **[U] on ClaimReg10, ClaimReg11 and ClaimReg12** — the entire financial and contractual picture is party-held and was not supplied — and **[V3] on Link4, Link5, Link6 and Link10**, the closure-spiral mechanisms, each carrying *analogy — no in-case support*. **[U] on ClaimReg13**, the jurisdiction, conditions every legal finding. This is evidence-quality transparency; no scored probability is implied or available.

**Action contingency — what must hold true after acting, distinct from what would flip the evidence.** The call succeeds only if: the finance function can actually produce per-site contribution figures within two weeks; the cash position tolerates a two-week delay; and the board can hold the two-week window without the decision leaking, since Node7 makes a leaked deliberation self-executing. Every premise could be [V1] and the call still fail on any of these three.

**(c) The dominant unknown.** The contribution each site makes — attributable income minus avoidable cost, plus that site's closure liabilities [ClaimReg10]. It is the one fact that would most move this analysis, and it is held by the charity, not by any source I can reach.

**(d) What to verify first.** One finance-officer day: last twelve months' income and directly attributable cost by site; each site's lease term, break clause and dilapidation estimate; redundancy cost by site; the restricted-funds note; and whether income is per-head or block-funded [ClaimReg11]. This is the cheapest step that most reduces risk, and it is what turns limb one of the call into a decision rather than a delay.

**(e) What would flip the call** — first-person conditionals.

- I am treating the deficit as structural [Assump1]; if it is a timing problem, the call reverses to "close nothing, manage the cash."
- I am treating income as not closely following attendance [Assump3, ClaimReg11]; if it does, closing the larger site loses more than it saves and limb two reverses.
- I am treating a whole-site closure as an unproven route rather than a settled one [Assump2, Find2]; if the board has already tested the alternatives at ClaimReg6 and documented it, limb one falls away and only limb two remains.
- I am treating no restricted fund or lease as tying the outcome [Assump4]; if one exists, it may decide the matter irrespective of this analysis.
- I am treating the reserve position as tolerating a two-week delay; if TipCond1 has already been crossed, the call weakens to "decide Friday and minute the absence of data."
- I am treating this as an England/Wales/Scotland charity [ClaimReg13]; if it is not, ClaimReg2, ClaimReg3 and ClaimReg4 do not apply and limb two loses most of its support.

**Dependence on the gate.** The operator recorded a position on **GateQ0 only** and then issued a standing deferral covering GateQ1 to GateQ5. **This call therefore rests on five default assumptions the operator has not endorsed**, named in the ledger above: that the binary is tested before it is answered; that financial survival governs over both headcount and depth of harm; that affected parties are consulted before announcement; that no loop is treated as dominant; and that the intervention level is the cost base and the funding model rather than the site count. **Answering any one of those questions voids this call, and the brief is re-derived.** In particular, an answer of "answer as asked" at GateQ1 removes limb one entirely, and an answer at GateQ2 that depth of harm outranks the survival criterion changes limb two's reasoning even where it does not change its direction.
