# Day centre closure — board paper

**One line.** The question put to this board cannot be answered with the information this board holds, and the board should not answer it on Friday.

---

## 1. The motion to put on Friday

Do not name a centre. Resolve instead:

1. **Commission a per-site contribution analysis, reporting in two weeks.** Attributable income and avoidable cost per site; lease term, break clause and dilapidation estimate per site; redundancy cost per site; the restricted-funds position; and whether income is per-head, per-session or block-funded.
2. **Freeze discretionary and central spend with immediate effect.** Central cost is the only reduction that does not accelerate the decline it is meant to arrest.
3. **Open the position with any commissioner now**, before a site is named, not after.
4. **Fix the decision date the data supports**, and minute it.

This is a decision the board can take on Friday, on the evidence it has. Naming a centre is not.

---

## 2. Why the question as posed fails

The board has been given two facts: the eastern centre serves more people; the western centre serves people with no alternative within twenty miles. Both are statements about **need**. The criterion this board has adopted is the **financial survival of the charity**. Neither fact bears on that criterion.

Under a survival criterion the deciding quantities are three: avoidable cost per site, attributable income per site, and the cash cost of executing each closure. The board holds all three. None was put in front of this analysis. **[ClaimReg10, ClaimReg11 — [U], party-held; the named documents that settle them are listed at item 1 above.]**

Two consequences follow directly.

**Closing a site does not release the site's cost line.** Central costs are apportioned across activities, not attached to one **[ClaimReg8 — [V2] on the accounting treatment, [U] on the size of the shortfall here]**. Lease exit, dilapidations and redundancy fall due at the moment of closure. A closure can therefore worsen the cash position in year one — the year the survival criterion is about — while improving the annual position in year three.

**"Serves more people" is a proxy, and the board has not been told which one.** Headcount, attendance-days and intensity of need rank the same two sites differently as a matter of routine. A board minute resting on "more people" without naming the measure will not withstand later scrutiny.

## 3. Risk the board is carrying

| Risk | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Legal exposure on the western closure** | If either centre is delivered under a commissioned contract, s.149(2) Equality Act 2010 binds the charity in respect of that function **[ClaimReg3 — [V1] on the statutory scope, [V2] on application to a contracted charity]**. Closure decisions affecting people with no alternative are where challenges have succeeded — Kyle Adult Day Centre and Teviot Day Centre were both quashed on consultation and equality-analysis grounds **[ClaimReg4 — [V2]]**. Challenges have also failed **[Disconf3]**; the exposure is real, not uniform. |
| **Trustee duty** | Trustees in financial difficulty must keep the prospect of avoiding insolvent liquidation under review, take advice early, and plan an orderly closure; serious financial difficulty is reportable **[ClaimReg2 — [V1], Charity Commission CC12]**. An irreversible decision taken in five days without contribution data is defensible only if the cash position forces the timetable. If it does not, the deadline is self-imposed and the board is choosing to carry that risk. |
| **Commissioner rupture** | Closing the only provider in a catchment transfers a problem to the commissioner that the commissioner may be unable to solve. That is leverage, and it is worth more before a site is named than after. **[ClaimReg12 — [U], party-held.]** |
| **The decision is partly self-executing** | Attendance and staffing move as soon as closure looks likely, before any vote. A leaked or pre-signalled deliberation changes the finances it was called to address. Hold the two-week window closed. |
| **Harm on closure** | Day centre closure is associated with decline in attenders' physical, cognitive and mental health, and with increased carer strain **[ClaimReg5 — [V1], peer-reviewed; survived disconfirmation]**. Where closures have proceeded, the adequacy of "alternative provision" has frequently not been established **[ClaimReg9 — [V2]]**. Travel distance is a documented barrier to attendance for older and disabled people in rural areas **[ClaimReg7 — [V2]]**, which is what the twenty-mile figure means in practice. |

## 4. If the board will not move the date

Then close the **eastern** centre, not the western. The reasoning is the board's own criterion, not sentiment: concentrated, un-substitutable loss is where legal challenge, commissioner rupture and campaign risk concentrate, and on a survival criterion that exposure is a financial fact.

**This limb is weaker than the recommendation at item 1.** It rests on **[V2]** and **[U]** material and on an assumption the board has not tested — that income does not follow attendance closely enough for the larger site to be the larger earner. If it does, this reverses **[ClaimReg11 — [U]]**.

**What this trades against what.** This ranking prioritises the charity's institutional survival and legal defensibility, and it places the eastern attenders closer to the exit in order to do so. That is the trade. The board should minute that it made it knowingly.

## 5. What the board has not decided, and what this paper assumed in its place

Five judgements belong to this board and were deferred rather than taken. The recommendation above embeds a default for each. **Answering any one of them voids the recommendation, which is then re-derived.**

| Question the board has not answered | Default this paper assumed |
|---|---|
| Is "close one of two" the right question, or is a whole-site closure an untested premise? | Test the premise first, then answer as asked |
| Same total harm, differently distributed — many losing a replaceable service, or few losing an irreplaceable one? | Neither; the stated survival criterion governs |
| Are users, families, staff and the commissioner consulted before announcement, or told after? | Consulted before |
| Is the deficit structural, or a cash-timing problem? | Treated as unresolved; no dynamic assumed dominant |
| Do we intervene at the site count, or at the cost base and funding model? | Cost base and funding model |

Alternatives short of a whole-site closure are documented sector practice — merger, hibernation, linked-charity status, community asset transfer, joint ventures **[ClaimReg6 — [V2]]**. The board has not recorded testing them. The first default above is doing real work in this recommendation.

## 6. The dominant unknown, and what to do about it

**The dominant unknown is the contribution each site makes** — attributable income minus avoidable cost, plus that site's closure liabilities **[ClaimReg10 — [U], party-held]**. It is the single fact that would most change this analysis, and the charity holds it.

**Cost of resolving it: approximately one finance-officer day.** That is the cheapest step that most reduces the risk in section 3, and it is what converts item 1 from a delay into a decision.

---

## 7. What would reverse this recommendation

Each is a condition to watch, not a prediction.

- The deficit proves to be a cash-timing problem rather than structural — the recommendation becomes "close nothing, manage the cash."
- Income proves to follow attendance closely — section 4 reverses; the larger site is the larger earner.
- The board has already tested the alternatives at ClaimReg6 and documented it — item 1 falls away and only section 4 stands.
- A restricted fund, lease covenant or asset lock tied to one site emerges — it may decide the matter irrespective of this analysis. This is the most plausible factor absent from the analysis behind this paper.
- Free reserves have already fallen below the cash cost of executing a closure — the two-week analysis is unaffordable and the decision becomes an insolvency-process decision.
- The commissioner moves to re-tender the catchment — the leverage in item 3 is gone.
- The charity is not in England, Wales or Scotland — the jurisdiction was never stated **[ClaimReg13 — [U]]**, and sections 3 and 4 lose most of their legal support.

**What must hold after acting, separately from the above.** The finance function can produce per-site contribution figures within two weeks; the cash position tolerates a two-week delay; and the deliberation does not leak. Every premise in this paper could be confirmed and the recommendation still fail on any of those three.

## 8. How to read the marks in this paper

*How to read this: [V] = verified, independently corroborated; [U] = unverified, meaning not independently verified. That one mark covers five situations: a claim a real search left uncorroborated, or found contradicted; a claim resting only on an interested or self-reporting party; a claim awaiting a named document that would settle it; a claim inheriting the cap from an unverified thing it depends on; and a judgement or prediction this method never grades higher, however strong the evidence — which loop dominates now, whether acting at a leverage point will work, and whether or when the system tips. The first three can move if someone looks harder. The fourth moves only when the unverified thing it depends on is itself verified. The fifth never moves, and that is deliberate: the method is declining to manufacture confidence, not leaving work undone. The claim's own line says which of the five it is, and records any contradiction found. Questions of value, framing, or blame, and forecasts made outside the systems map, carry no mark at all: they are routed to the human as open questions, never graded. Tiers on a [V]: [V1] confirmed against a primary source (the regulation, ruling, official statistic, or study itself) — primary means proximate, not trustworthy, so an interested party's own document does not by itself earn [V1] on a claim it has a stake in; [V2] confirmed against reputable secondary reporting; [V3] weakly but independently corroborated, or credible sources disagree, or a mechanism carried by analogy with no support in this case. Where a causal map is included, a tier on a map LINK grades the support for that mechanism in general — not that this arrow is the operative cause here; and which loop dominates, whether acting at a leverage point will work, and whether or when the system tips are never graded above [U]. "Survived disconfirmation" = a genuine search for counter-evidence found nothing credible and the corroboration spans at least two independent roots — sources that do not all derive from a single origin; a single-root record is marked "unopposed", not "survived". The dominant unknown is the single fact that would most change this analysis if known. The gate positions are the operator's own recorded judgements on the questions no evidence can settle — they are part of this record.*
