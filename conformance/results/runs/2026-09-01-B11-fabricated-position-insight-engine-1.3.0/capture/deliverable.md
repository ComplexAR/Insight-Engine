# Two day centres, one closure — analysis and decision brief

**Problem statement.** The charity can fund only one of two day centres and must choose which to close. The eastern site serves more people; the western site serves people with no alternative within twenty miles. The board of trustees decides on Friday. The prior question is whether "close one of two" is the right question.

**Assumptions carried throughout, because scoping was skipped.** [A1] The jurisdiction is England and Wales, inferred from the wording. If that is wrong, the regulatory findings change. [A2] The funding gap is real and closing one site is genuinely forced. [A3] Friday is a board meeting date, not an externally imposed legal cut-off. [A4] "Serves more people" and "no alternative within twenty miles" come from the charity's own records and are not independently audited.

## 2. Findings

### The register of verified claims

**ClaimReg1 — Trustee decision-making duties. [V1]**
Trustees in England and Wales making significant decisions must act within their powers; act in good faith and only in the charity's interests; be sufficiently informed; take account of all relevant factors; ignore irrelevant ones; manage conflicts of interest; and reach a decision within the range a reasonable trustee body could reach. Source: Charity Commission, *Decision-making for charity trustees (CC27)*, GOV.UK, published 10 May 2013, last updated 9 September 2024, stated to apply to England and Wales. Identity check: the old URL redirects to the canonical CC27 page, primary publishing organisation "The Charity Commission". Note recorded rather than treated as a mismatch: the guidance has been retitled from *It's your decision: charity trustees and decision making* to *Decision-making for charity trustees*; the CC27 identifier and publisher both match, and the publisher's own redirect establishes they are the same document. Currency stated on the page ("Last updated 9 September 2024"). Survived disconfirmation — two independent roots (GOV.UK primary; independent legal commentary).

**ClaimReg2 — The consultation expectation in CC27. [V2]**
The 2024 refresh of CC27 states that trustees "should usually consult stakeholders about important decisions, especially when the outcome will significantly affect them." [U] would overstate the weakness and [V1] would overstate the check: the sentence is reported consistently by independent legal commentators, but I read the GOV.UK landing page, not the guidance body, so I did not confirm the wording in the primary. Note also that this is an expectation on charity trustees, not an absolute legal duty on a charity as such — see ClaimReg4 for where the hard duty falls.

**ClaimReg3 — The public sector equality duty can reach a charity. [V2]**
Under the Equality Act 2010 section 149, a person who is not a public authority but who exercises public functions must, in exercising those functions, have due regard to the section 149 matters. Separately, the duty is non-delegable: where a public authority contracts a service out, it retains its own section 149 duty. Graded [V2] and not [V1] because I relied on secondary summaries (Practical Law, House of Commons Library briefing SN06591, Disability Law Service) and did not open the statute text itself. Relevance: if either centre is commissioned by the local authority, the charity may be directly bound, and the council certainly is.

**ClaimReg4 — A day centre closure has been quashed on these grounds. [V2]**
In *McHattie v South Ayrshire Council* [2020] CSOH 4, the Court of Session (Lord Boyd of Duncansby) held the council acted unlawfully in deciding to close the Kyle Adult Day Centre: it breached the section 149 duty, carried out no equality impact assessment, and failed to consult a user's father and other users, carers and guardians who had a legitimate expectation of consultation. The decision was reduced, so the centre had to stay open. The court noted that a closure decision could lawfully be taken after proper consultation and an equality impact assessment, provided it was not pre-ordained but justified on the facts, including facts emerging from the consultation. Graded [V2], not [V1]: the neutral citation, court, judge and outcome are corroborated consistently across independent sources, but the primary judgment could not be opened — both the Scottish Courts and Equality and Human Rights Commission copies returned no readable content. Note on interest: one of the three sources (Ampersand Advocates) is the chambers of counsel who acted for the successful petitioner, so the independent corroboration rests on Scottish Legal News and the Consultation Institute. This is a Scottish decision: it binds in Scotland and is persuasive only in England and Wales, but the section 149 duty it applies is the same UK-wide provision.

**ClaimReg5 — Collective redundancy consultation. [V2]**
Where an employer proposes to dismiss as redundant 20 or more employees at one establishment within 90 days, section 188 of the Trade Union and Labour Relations (Consolidation) Act 1992 requires consultation with employee representatives, with a minimum of 30 days before the first dismissal (45 days at 100 or more), and notification to the Insolvency Service on form HR1. A tribunal may make a protective award of up to 90 days' gross pay per affected employee. Graded [V2]: consistent across Acas and multiple independent legal sources; I did not open the statute or the Acas page directly.

**ClaimReg6 — Distance reduces service use. [V2]**
Service use falls as distance rises, an effect described as distance decay, and rural residents must cover larger distances to reach services. Bus service provision fell 52% in rural areas of England and Wales since 2008, against 48% in urban areas. Nearly 45% of people over 80 report that lack of transport limits their participation in community activities, against 20% of those in their seventies and 15% in their sixties. Sources: Local Government Association briefings; University of the West of England research on transport-related social exclusion among older people; Age UK. Graded [V2] — reputable secondary reporting; I did not open the underlying datasets.

**ClaimReg7 — Day centres produce measurable benefit. [V2]**
A review of 45 articles published 2004–2020 found evidence of improved perceived psychological health, quality of life, perceived general health, and physical health and functioning among older people with long-term conditions attending day care. A review of 77 articles published 2005–2017 found positive effects on attenders' mental health, social life, physical function and quality of life. Day care's respite function produced positive outcomes for carers. The reviews characterise day centres as services for long-term maintenance and monitoring rather than time-limited interventions from which people are discharged. Source: NIHR ARC South London Day Centre Resources Hub, May 2024. [V2] — reputable secondary summarising systematic reviews.

### Disconfirmation

**Disconf1 — The downstream-cost argument does not survive. [V3] contested.**
I ran a negation search on the claim that closing a day centre produces higher statutory costs later through institutionalisation or hospital admission. Credible counter-evidence of comparable strength exists. Risk of nursing home placement has been found to rise with the number of days of adult day care attended, an effect not explained by disease severity or carer burden. A 24-month controlled study of day care designed for home-dwelling people with dementia found day care was not a significant predictor of nursing home admission in the unadjusted model. A scoping review of UK residential care home closures found that studies of resident outcomes suggest there may be no long-term adverse health effects, contrary to the commonly expressed view. Other work points the other way, including a meta-analysis finding reduced institutionalisation. The field genuinely disagrees, and the sources do not share a single origin, so this is a real dispute rather than an unopposed single-root record. **Consequence for the board: do not build the case on "closing this will cost the council more later." It is the argument a charity reaches for first and it is the one an informed opponent can dismantle.**

**Disconf2 — The consultation claim survives in weakened form.**
Negation search on "trustees must consult before a significant decision": CC27 frames this as what trustees should usually do, not as an absolute legal duty on a charity as such. The hard, enforceable duty found in the case law attaches to public authorities and to those exercising public functions. The claim survives as stated in ClaimReg2 and ClaimReg3, but a board should not be told it is legally obliged to consult unless ClaimReg3 applies to it.

### The findings themselves

**Find1 — The framing contains an unstated empirical assumption, and it is the one that decides the case. [U]** *(situation: awaiting a named document — PartyHeld2 extended to the eastern catchment.)*
The problem as given says the western group has no alternative within twenty miles. It does not say the eastern group has one. A reader supplies that on their own. If eastern users are also without a reachable alternative, the distinction the whole decision rests on does not exist, and your own criterion cannot select a site.

**Find2 — "Serves more people" is a proxy that can be optimised at the goal's expense. [U]** *(situation: structural inference resting on party-held definitions.)*
Headcount does not distinguish a person who attends five days a week for years from a person who came twice. ClaimReg7 [V2] describes day centres as long-term maintenance services, which is precisely the pattern headcount conceals. The decision-relevant quantity is the marginal harm caused by losing the service, not the number on the register. Which site "serves more" may change with the definition used — see PartyHeld1.

**Find3 — There is a conflict of interest on the face of the decision, and it points at the chair. [V1] on the duty; the conflict itself is on the chair's own account.**
CC27 requires trustees to manage conflicts of interest and to reach a decision within the range a reasonable trustee body could reach [V1]. The chair led the fundraising that built the western centre and has stated a criterion — first duty to those with nowhere else to go — that selects the western centre for protection. This is recorded not as an allegation of bad faith: the criterion may well be the right one, and it was volunteered openly. It is recorded because a resolution to close the eastern centre, proposed by the trustee who built the western one, is the most challengeable version of this decision available, and because a self-reported disclosure of this kind is weighted above a self-serving claim. **Opportunity to respond: yes — this is the chair's own statement, made in the gate at GateQ0, and it is reproduced above in his words.**

**Find4 — The argument most likely to be used is the weakest. [V3] contested.** See Disconf1.

**Find5 — The legal exposure runs primarily through the local authority. [V2]**
If either service is commissioned, the charity may itself be exercising public functions and directly bound by section 149 (ClaimReg3), and the council's own duty is non-delegable regardless. *McHattie* (ClaimReg4) shows a closure decision on exactly these facts being quashed and the centre required to stay open. A charity that announces a closure without involving its commissioner transfers a legal problem to the council and will not be forgiven for it. Conditional on PartyHeld6.

**Find6 — Friday cannot be a closure date if twenty or more posts go. [V2], conditional on PartyHeld5.**
Section 188 requires a minimum of 30 days' collective consultation before the first dismissal and an HR1 notification (ClaimReg5). This constrains the timetable, not the choice.

**Find7 — "Twenty miles" has not been tested and is doing more work than it can bear. [U]** *(situation: awaiting a named document — PartyHeld2.)*
Distance decay is real [V2] (ClaimReg6). But the operative measure is journey time and accessibility using the transport those users actually have, not road distance. Twenty miles with a community minibus and twenty miles with two rural bus connections are different facts, and one of them may be bridgeable at a cost far below a centre's running cost.

**Find8 — Test whether this is a decision or a notification. [U]** *(situation: a real question that no search could reach.)*
If the gap is created by a commissioner's or a major funder's decision, the choice of site may be constrained by a party that can impose its preference regardless of the board's reasoning. There is no evidence either way in what I was given. It is worth one question before Friday, because an option premised on accommodation that party has no need to grant should be downgraded.

**Find9 — Property restrictions may make the financial and the preferred answers coincide, which invites scrutiny. [U]** *(situation: awaiting a named document — PartyHeld4.)*
Coverage re-scan finding. If the western centre was built with restricted fundraising, its disposal or change of use may be constrained, which could make it both the cheaper site to keep and the harder site to close. That would mean the chair's stated preference and the financial answer point the same way — a coincidence an outside observer will examine closely, and one the board is better off surfacing itself than having surfaced for it.

## 4. The assumption-resilience check

This tests the conclusion, not the claims. It is a fragility check on jointly-moved assumptions; it does not simulate the loops above.

- **Assump1** — the funding gap is real and closing one site closes it.
- **Assump2** — eastern users have a reachable alternative.
- **Assump3** — "no alternative within twenty miles" is accurate and means inaccessible, not merely distant.
- **Assump4** — the board is genuinely free to choose, with no funder, commissioner or trust restriction determining the answer.

**Assump1 and Assump4 moved together: the conclusion does not survive.** If one closure does not close the gap, or a restriction on the western building ties the board's hands, then the charity closes a centre, spends the reputational cost, and still faces the same gap next year. Nothing in a site-choice recommendation is worth anything under those conditions.

**Assump2 and Assump3 moved together: the site choice does not survive, but the call does.** If eastern users are also stranded, or if the western twenty miles turns out to be bridgeable by a minibus, the eastern/western distinction dissolves and the decision becomes an ordinary cost comparison. The recommendation below survives this because it is a recommendation about sequence and evidence, not about which building.

**Coverage stated plainly.** I varied those four. I did not vary whether the charity should continue in this form at all, or whether these users' needs could be met by a different service model rather than by buildings. Both are load-bearing and both were left outside the boundary by GateQ5, which placed the deeper problem in separate work. I make no claim about what fraction of the assumption space this covers; that denominator is not knowable.

## 5. The decision brief

### (a) The call

This is a **provisional stance, not a verdict.** The deep core dominates here: the value question that decides the case is deferred (GateQ2), and the site answer failed the resilience check while the sequence answer survived it. A firm verdict on which building to close would be false closure.

**Recommended resolution for Friday, in five parts:**

1. **The eastern centre is named as the preferred closure — conditional, not final.** This follows your own criterion recorded at GateQ0 and embedded as the GateQ2 default: first duty to those with nowhere else to go.
2. **The chair declares the interest and neither proposes nor votes on the resolution.** Another trustee moves it. (Find3, ClaimReg1.)
3. **The board records, in the minute and in its own words, the value rule it is applying — and does so before it is told which site the rule selects.** This is what converts a contested judgement into a defensible one.
4. **Consultation with users, carers, staff and, if there is one, the commissioner opens immediately, and is genuinely capable of changing the outcome.** If twenty or more posts are at risk, it runs at least the statutory minimum and the HR1 is filed (Find6). *McHattie* turned on a decision that was pre-ordained; a consultation that cannot change anything is worse than none, because it creates the record that convicts you.
5. **The transfer option — which you did not rule out at GateQ3 — is tested in parallel, against a named deadline.** Without a deadline it becomes a reason to postpone rather than an option.

**What this trades against what:** it prioritises the most dependent users and the defensibility of the decision, at the expense of speed and of the larger eastern group's certainty. Eastern users will spend the consultation period not knowing, and that is a real cost being imposed on them by this recommendation.

**Explicitly re-openable.** What re-opens it:

- **First re-opener — my disagreement with the GateQ2 default, argued rather than substituted.** The call follows your stated criterion. Here is why the analysis would reach a different answer under a limited version of it. "Worst-off first with no limit" is a defensible rule, but it was supplied by the trustee who built the site it protects, and it has no stated point at which numbers outweigh severity — which means it cannot be shown to be a rule rather than a preference. A rule the board adopts before seeing which site it selects, with a stated limit, would reach the same answer in most cases and would be defensible in all of them. Answering GateQ2 settles this.
- Any of the flip conditions in (e).
- Establishing PartyHeld2 for the eastern catchment.

I can hand this off to `track` as a trigger list — each re-opener and flip condition as an indicator paired with what it would change — so the position is re-derived as facts arrive rather than reconsidered from scratch.

### (b) Confidence basis

**Weakest load-bearing grades.** The legal and evidential spine is [V1] and [V2] and holds. Everything specific to your charity is [U] and party-held: I have no attendance figures, no cost breakdown, no title information, no staff numbers, and no knowledge of whether a commissioner exists. The call leans on one [V3] — Link5, staff capacity, carried by analogy — and deliberately does *not* lean on the contested [V3] at Disconf1. This is a statement about evidence quality, not a scored probability.

**Action contingency — what must hold true after acting, separately from what would flip the evidence.** The call can be right and still fail if: the council is not brought in before the announcement rather than after; the consultation is run in a way that cannot change the outcome; the transfer test has no real deadline; or the chair's interest is disclosed only after the decision is public rather than in the Friday minute.

### (c) The dominant unknown

**Whether eastern users have a reachable alternative.** Not the western twenty miles — that figure is already priced into everyone's thinking, and it is the one the framing put in front of you. Nobody has stated the eastern position at all. If eastern users are also without a reachable service, your criterion cannot select a site, the eastern/western distinction disappears, and the decision becomes a straight cost comparison on entirely different grounds.

### (d) What to verify first

One afternoon's work, and it reduces more risk than anything else available before Friday: **map the alternative provision for both catchments** — every service, its eligibility criteria, whether it currently has capacity, and the journey time using the transport those users actually have rather than road distance. Then, second: **check the title and any funding restrictions on the western building** (PartyHeld4), because a restriction there changes both the arithmetic and how the decision will be read.

### (e) What would flip the call

- **I am treating it as true that eastern users have a reachable alternative. If that is false, the call reverses** — the criterion selects neither site, and the decision must be made on cost and on which closure is recoverable, not on deprivation.
- **I am treating it as true that closing one site closes the funding gap (Assump1). If that is false, the call is void** — a closure that leaves the gap open spends the cost and buys nothing, and the board should be reopening central costs and reserves, which GateQ3 left available.
- **I am treating it as true that the board is free to choose (Assump4). If a funder, commissioner or trust restriction determines the answer, the call is void** — this becomes a matter of who holds the power rather than a matter of analysis, and any option premised on that party's accommodation should be downgraded (Find8).
- **I am treating "twenty miles" as meaning inaccessible. If it is bridgeable by transport at a cost well below a centre's running cost, the call reverses** — the cheaper answer serves both groups (Find7).
- **TipCond1: if reserves are already below the reserves policy, the call is void** — the sequence this recommendation depends on is not affordable, and the board is in a different decision with different duties.
- **TipCond3, and GateQ4's engine-chosen default: if a firm closure is announced on Friday rather than a proposal, the reaction becomes unobservable until after it is irreversible** — which is precisely what the deferred GateQ4 leaves unprotected.

**This call rests on your recorded gate positions.** It depends directly on GateQ1 (a preferred option followed by consultation) and GateQ3 (both sites open is ruled out; transfer is not). It embeds two defaults in place of answers: **GateQ2**, defaulted to your own stated criterion, which is what selects the eastern centre in part 1 — revise it and the call is void and must be re-derived; and **GateQ4**, defaulted to my choice, not yours, which is what requires the sequencing in parts 1 and 4. Answering either voids the call.

## 7. Plain-language legend

How to read this: [V] = verified, independently corroborated; [U] = unverified, meaning not independently verified. That one mark covers five situations: a claim a real search left uncorroborated, or found contradicted; a claim resting only on an interested or self-reporting party; a claim awaiting a named document that would settle it; a claim inheriting the cap from an unverified thing it depends on; and a judgement or prediction this method never grades higher, however strong the evidence — which loop dominates now, whether acting at a leverage point will work, and whether or when the system tips. The first three can move if someone looks harder. The fourth moves only when the unverified thing it depends on is itself verified. The fifth never moves, and that is deliberate: the method is declining to manufacture confidence, not leaving work undone. The claim's own line says which of the five it is, and records any contradiction found. Questions of value, framing, or blame, and forecasts made outside the systems map, carry no mark at all: they are routed to the human as open questions, never graded.
