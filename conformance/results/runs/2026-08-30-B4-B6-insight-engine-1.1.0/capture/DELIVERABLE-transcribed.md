### 2.1 The verification register

**ClaimReg1.** The "contribution condition" in Schedule 8 — the landlord group net worth test of N × £2,000,000 — **does not apply** where the relevant landlord at the qualifying time was a private registered provider of social housing. `[V1]` Building Safety Act 2022 (2022 c. 30), Schedule 8 paragraph 3(6)(a), revised text in force on or before 28 August 2026, opened and identity-checked at legislation.gov.uk. *Contradicted the secondary sources retrieved, which described the net-worth test as applying to landlords generally; primary text preferred.*

**ClaimReg2.** Schedule 8 paragraph 2: "No service charge is payable under the lease in respect of a relevant measure relating to a relevant defect if a relevant landlord (a) is responsible for the relevant defect". Paragraph 2(3)(b): a person is responsible if "the person undertook or commissioned works relating to the defect". This applies to **a lease of any premises in a relevant building** — not only to qualifying leases. `[V1]` same source, opened and quoted. Application to this block: `[U]` — *awaiting a named document that would settle it* (the overcladding contract and procurement record).

**ClaimReg3.** Schedule 8 paragraph 8: "No service charge is payable under a qualifying lease in respect of cladding remediation", where cladding remediation means removal or replacement of part of a cladding system that "(a) forms the outer wall of an external wall system, and (b) is unsafe". `[V1]` same source, opened and quoted. Whether combustible insulation and cavity barriers sitting **behind** the rainscreen fall inside paragraph 8, or instead inside the capped non-cladding regime, is arguable: `[U]` — *awaiting legal determination on this building*.

**ClaimReg4.** Permitted maximum outside Greater London: £10,000 (paragraph 6(2)(b)). Annual limit: one tenth of the permitted maximum (paragraph 7(1)). ### 2.3 The findings

**Find1 — The largest cost in the frame is the one none of the three options addresses.** All three options pay for interim measures out of the association's own money. A fund exists whose stated purpose is to stop that: IMAF, live since 1 April 2026, grant-funds the capital cost of a common alarm to replace a waking watch `[V1] ClaimReg7`. On `ClaimReg11` the alarm that displaces a £310,000 annual cost has a capital cost in the order of £15,000–£50,000. Applied to this block the arithmetic is `[U]` — *inherits the cap from party-held cost figures* — but the direction is not in doubt. A waking watch running for three years costs roughly £930,000, close to two thirds of the entire partial scheme, and buys no permanent risk reduction at all.

**Find2 — Displacement. Each option moves the cost onto someone not in the room.** "Start now from reserves" displaces onto the future tenants of the 90 social rent homes, who are not yet an identifiable group and cannot object. "Wait for the fund" displaces onto the 62 households, who carry the risk and the disruption for an undefined period. "Partial scheme" displaces onto the future: £1.6m spent, the insulation left, and on `ClaimReg18 [V3]` a possible insurance and valuation problem acquired in exchange, plus the likelihood of a second scaffold later.

**Find3 — Where the £310,000 came from.** The figure is the contractor's, and the contractor is the party who would be paid it. A contractor pricing a waking watch has no reason to volunteer that a grant-funded alarm would end the need for it. This is not an allegation of bad faith; it is a statement about the evidential weight of the number. **The contractor was not offered the opportunity to respond to this finding.**

**Find4 — Conspicuously absent.** A thorough treatment of this decision would normally contain, and this one does not: the date and procurer of the rainscreen; a recovery strategy against whoever installed it; the funding-route question (Building Safety Fund or Cladding Safety Scheme); the insurance position; the leaseholders' statutory position; the notification position with the Regulator of Social Housing and the Building Safety Regulator; the fire and rescue service's view; and any communication with residents at all. The most consequential omission is the first.

**Find5 — On leaseholders, the board is asking the wrong question.** The question is not "can we charge them" but "did we commission the works that created the defect". If the association undertook or commissioned the overcladding, Schedule 8 paragraph 2 removes the service charge entirely — for every lease in the building, not only qualifying leases, and for every relevant measure relating to that defect, not only the cladding element `[V1] ClaimReg2`. Contrary agreements are void `[V1] ClaimReg6`. Application here is `[U]` pending PartyHeld2.

**Find6 — Two things the board may be assuming that the statute does not support.** First, the £2m net-worth contribution condition does not apply to a private registered provider `[V1] ClaimReg1`, so it is neither the reason the association can charge nor the reason it cannot. Second, the cladding exemption is drafted around the part of the system that "forms the outer wall" `[V1] ClaimReg3`; combustible insulation and cavity barriers sit behind it, and whether their removal is inside or outside that exemption is unresolved `[U]`.

**Find7 — For many of these leaseholders the cap may be nil regardless.** Outside Greater London the permitted maximum is £10,000, the annual limit £1,000, and it is zero where the lease value at the qualifying time was under £175,000 `[V1] ClaimReg4`. A right-to-buy flat in a 1970s block in the north of England may well fall below that threshold. `[U]` pending PartyHeld3. The two leaseholders who have asked can be given a truthful, reassuring and legally careful answer this week; they cannot be given a final figure.

**Find8 — The silence is now a funding risk, not only a relationship risk.** IMAF makes evidence of resident and leaseholder communication a term of the funding, at application and again at completion `[V1] ClaimReg8`. "Residents have been told nothing" is an obstacle to the cheapest available intervention, not a gap that can be closed afterwards.

**Find9 — Regulatory exposure is already running.** A registered provider must promptly inform the Regulator of Social Housing of a potential material issue, and RSH passes building safety concerns to the Building Safety Regulator for higher-risk buildings `[V2] ClaimReg17`. An eleven-storey block is above the seven-storey threshold. A known defect with no plan and no resident communication is the kind of issue a regulator learns about from a resident rather than from the board.

**Find10 — This is partly a power problem, not an analysis problem.** Government sets the funding rules, Homes England decides the application, and on `ClaimReg12 [V3]` a statutory duty with criminal penalties may fix the deadline whatever the board decides. The board's real discretion is over sequencing and cash flow, not over whether the work happens. Any option premised on the fund answering on a timetable that suits the board should be discounted: the fund has no need to grant that.

**Find11 — Base rate on the fund.** Of 3,071 buildings registered with the Building Safety Fund and not proceeding, 1,633 withdrew and 1,118 were ineligible `[V2] ClaimReg14`. An application with no decision and no date sits in a population where withdrawal and ineligibility are common outcomes. This weakens "wait for the fund"; it does not settle it.

**Find12 — Reflexive.** The way this was handed over — three costed options, a deadline, a request for a recommendation — invites the selection of one. The most useful thing that can be said is that the option set is incomplete, and that the two highest-value moves are not options at all but actions available this week under any of the three.

**Find13 — Duties already in force, independent of any funding choice.** An eleven-storey occupied building is above the seven-storey threshold, which brings Part 4 duties: registration with the Building Safety Regulator, a safety case report, and a **resident engagement strategy**. "Residents have been told nothing" is therefore not only a trust problem and a funding-term problem; it may be an existing compliance failure that no option on the table addresses. `[V2]` — added on adjudication; the Part 4 duties were not separately verified at primary source in this run, so treat the specific list as indicative and check it.

**Find14 — The recovery route runs in the association's favour, not only against it.** Contribution orders under section 124 can be sought **by** the association, and the extended retrospective limitation may reach a defective overcladding contract depending on its date `[V2] ClaimReg15`. This is a fourth funding route absent from the three options, and it is gated by exactly the fact named as the dominant unknown. If the overcladding pre-dates the retrospective window, or the commissioning party is dissolved, it closes.

**Find15 — Any leaseholder-charging pathway is procedurally incomplete on current facts.** Beyond Schedule 8, charging would require section 20 consultation and the landlord's certificate and leaseholder deed machinery. None of that has been started. This matters less as a legal obstacle than as a signal: the board is weighing a charging question it is not currently in a position to act on either way.

## 4. Assumption-resilience check

The call rests most on four assumptions. Varied together:

- `Assump1` — the association commissioned the overcladding, so Schedule 8 paragraph 2 engages and a recovery route may exist.
- `Assump2` — the block is IMAF-eligible and a panel supplier can install within months.
- `Assump3` — the £310,000 is a genuinely avoidable recurring cost, not partly fixed.
- `Assump4` — the 90-home programme can be re-phased without losing grant.

**Result.** Moving `Assump2` and `Assump3` false together is what breaks the call: if the waking watch cannot be displaced, the interim cost is a fixed £310,000 a year and the decision collapses back to a straight reserves question, in which waiting is expensive and the partial scheme becomes less obviously wrong. Moving `Assump1` false removes the leaseholder answer and the recovery route but does not change the sequencing recommendation. Moving `Assump4` false makes the opportunity-cost question sharper but does not reverse the call.

**Coverage.** Varied together: `Assump1`–`Assump4`. Not varied: whether the £4.8m quote is sound, whether any partial scheme would satisfy the FRAEW assessor, and whether borrowing is available at all. No claim is made about what proportion of the assumption space this covers; that denominator is not knowable.

## 5. Decision brief

### (a) The call — a provisional stance, not a verdict

**Recommend that the board adopt none of the three options as framed, and instead decide on a sequence.**

This is a wicked problem: the deep core dominates, all six gate lines were defaulted, and there is no verifiable solution that the evidence alone selects. The stance is therefore explicitly re-openable.

Before the meeting, and independent of which option is eventually chosen:

1. **Apply to IMAF for a common alarm** and instruct the fire risk assessor to confirm what would be needed to stand the waking watch down. `LevPoint1`.
2. **Write to all 62 households**, and answer the two leaseholders with the statutory position rather than a figure. `LevPoint2`. This is a term of the funding, not only a courtesy.
3. **Establish who commissioned the rainscreen, and when.** `LevPoint3`. This single fact determines the leaseholder answer and whether a recovery route exists.
4. **Confirm with Homes England which scheme this block belongs in**, and whether the undecided Building Safety Fund application should have transferred. `LevPoint4`.
5. **Notify the Regulator of Social Housing** if that has not already happened. `Find9`.

**Amended after adjudication and re-verification.** At the meeting the recommendation is **a decision rule, not a spending commitment**: authorise the cavity-barrier works as the first phase of a full scheme, subject to two written conditions — confirmation from Homes England that commencing does not prejudice the application for the insulation, and the fire risk assessor's confirmation of the post-works rating. **Do not sign a remediation works contract until the funding route is confirmed in writing.** On `ClaimReg19 [V3]`, signing early may forfeit the grant rather than merely delay it, which would convert a £4.8m timing question into a £4.8m permanent cost.

My first draft recommended committing to full remediation from reserves at this meeting. That was sequenced backwards against my own pre-meeting step 4: the scheme question gates the decision, it does not decorate it. I have withdrawn it.

**I also withdraw the strike-out of the partial scheme.** I had rejected it partly on the insurance and valuation problem, which the spine grades `[V3]`, single-root and unopposed — the exact unearned weight this method exists to catch, and it was caught. `ClaimReg18 [V2]` cuts the other way: under PAS 9980 an assessor may find the insulation tolerable once the cavity barriers are addressed. The partial scheme is not the weakest option. It is the right first phase **if and only if** it is grant-protected and assessor-gated, and the wrong one if those conditions are treated as paperwork.

**The trade-off this embeds.** On the current default it prioritises the 62 existing households over the delivery timetable for the 90 planned social rent homes. That is GateQ2. It was defaulted, re-opened after adjudication, and defaulted again. **It is the largest resource-allocation and values decision available to this association, and no one has owned it.** A board reading this should understand that adopting the recommendation adopts that trade-off, and that the trade-off is the board's to make, not the analysis's. If the board disagrees, nothing else here needs to change — the pre-meeting sequence holds under any answer.

**Re-openers, as a trigger list for a living workspace:**

- IMAF application refused → the interim cost is fixed; re-derive.
- Overcladding found to have been commissioned by a third party still in existence → recovery route opens; the reserve question changes shape.
- Overcladding found to have been commissioned by the association → Schedule 8 paragraph 2 engages; leaseholders charged nothing; the recovery route closes.
- Fire risk assessor declines to stand down the waking watch after alarm installation → `Assump2` and `Assump3` fall together; the call breaks.
- Other blocks in the stock found to need the same work → the missing driver is real; the whole frame is wrong.
- Remediation Bill enacted with the 2029 deadline → `TipCond3` becomes live; sequencing stops being discretionary.

### (b) Confidence basis, and the action contingency

Confidence in the fact chain is high where it matters most and weak where it is most contingent on politics. The two cheapest checks are also the two highest-value ones, and neither costs money: **the overcladding procurement record**, held in the association's own files, and **an IMAF eligibility check** with Homes England.
