# Which day centre closes — a decision brief for Friday's board

**Problem statement.** A charity under cost pressure must choose which of two day centres to close — the eastern one serving more people, the western one serving people with no alternative within twenty miles — with the trustee board taking the decision on Friday, the executive team implementing it, and a commissioner or funder also reading the recommendation.

**Assumptions carried, because they were not supplied.** Jurisdiction is England or Wales (inferred from "charity", trustees, "day centre", miles). The shortfall's size, the reserves position, and the saving each closure yields are unknown to me. Attendance figures, dependency mix, cost per place, staff headcount and actual journey times are unknown to me. The operator stated that nothing is fixed and nothing is out of scope, so options beyond the two-centre binary were treated as in scope.

### 2.2 What the framing displaces, and onto whom

The comparison as handed over measures throughput on one side and distance on the other, and measures nothing at all on the third side, where the costs actually land.

Closing the western centre displaces cost onto: the users themselves; their unpaid carers, who lose the respite that is the best-evidenced benefit in ClaimReg7; the local authority, which retains its Care Act duties and will meet the need through home care, transport or out-of-area placement; and primary and emergency care. **The charity's saving is in significant part a cost transfer, not a cost elimination.** Where the commissioner is the payer, the charity may be handing its own funder a larger bill without telling it — which is a relationship fact as much as a financial one.

Closing the eastern centre displaces cost onto more people, each of whom has an alternative — lower harm per head, higher aggregate disruption — and onto the charity's own revenue base and scale economies, which is the second-order trap: the closure that looks kinder this year can make the surviving site unviable next year.

### 2.4 This is partly a power problem, not only an analysis problem

If a commissioner or funder has withdrawn or frozen money and the charity is absorbing the shortfall internally, then the charity is choosing which of its own beneficiaries bears a loss that a better-resourced party created. The framing "which of our two centres closes" makes that party invisible. **Any option premised on the commissioner voluntarily restoring funding is downgraded accordingly: the commissioner has no need to grant it, and a plan that depends on the goodwill of the party that caused the shortfall is not a plan.** The commissioner's Care Act s.5 duty (ClaimReg3) is a lever the charity can use, but it is a duty owed to the market and to service sufficiency, not a promise to this charity.

### 2.5 What I am setting aside

I am setting aside the argument that the western centre must be preserved because its users would be devastated. The devastation is plausible and the carer-respite loss is well-evidenced, but ClaimReg7 shows the outcome literature on day-centre attendance is weaker than advocates generally assume, and an argument that overstates its evidential base will be taken apart by anyone motivated to do so. The stronger version of the same point is structural, not emotive: the western users' need does not vanish, it transfers, and it transfers to a body under a statutory duty (ClaimReg3).

**Adverse findings and right of reply.** No finding here is adverse to a named individual. The finding at 2.4 is adverse to the commissioner or funder as an institution; that party was **not offered the opportunity to respond**, because this analysis was run from one side only, and the finding should be read with that limitation attached.

## 4. The decision brief

### (a) The call

**A provisional stance, not a verdict — this is a wicked problem and the call is explicitly re-openable.**

Recommend to Friday's board that it resolves three things rather than one:

1. **Name the eastern centre as the provisional preferred closure**, subject to consultation — not as a decision.
2. **Open a consultation of four to six weeks** that meets the formative-stage, sufficient-reasons, adequate-time and conscientious-consideration tests (ClaimReg5), with an equality impact assessment run alongside it (ClaimReg1), and take the final decision at a reconvened meeting.
3. **Commission, before that meeting, the one number that decides it**: the twelve-month net saving of each closure (ClaimReg9), together with the objects-and-restrictions check (ClaimReg10) and the staff count against the amended s.188 trigger (ClaimReg4).

**The value this trades.** It prioritises avoiding irreversible harm to the western users, who on the framing given have nowhere else to go, at the expense of aggregate reach and at the expense of some risk to the charity's larger revenue base. **That trade runs against the operator's own scoping answer**, which named the charity's survival as the heaviest interest, and against the default embedded at GateQ2. The conflict is real and is stated rather than resolved, because the question that resolves it was refused.

**Why not simply "close the western one and be done".** Because the need transfers rather than disappearing (ClaimReg3), because the transfer is to a body under a statutory duty which will notice the bill (Link2), and because the harm is irreversible in a way the eastern closure's is not.

**Why not refuse the binary altogether.** GateQ1 was refused, and the default embedded there requires the call to name a centre. The alternatives at 2.3 item 8 are recorded as options the board has not costed, not as a substitute for the recommendation.

**Re-openers, as a trigger list `track` could watch** (indicator → what it would change):

- Twelve-month net saving of each closure, once produced → may reverse which centre is named.
- Objects, restricted funds or title conditions naming a district or building → may remove the choice entirely.
- Staff headcount at risk across both sites against s.188/s.195A → may make any Friday timetable unlawful.
- Free reserves against the minimum reserves policy (TipCond1) → may convert this from a discretionary choice into solvency management.
- Any external announcement or briefing already made (TipCond4) → may make the consultation recommendation unavailable.
- Commissioner signalling decommissioning rather than re-tender (TipCond2) → may moot the comparison.

### (b) Confidence basis, and the action contingency

**Confidence in the fact chain.** The legal spine is strong: ClaimReg1, ClaimReg3 and ClaimReg4 are `[V1]`, retrieved from the statute and identity-checked on 2026-08-31. The consultation spine is `[V2]` (ClaimReg5, ClaimReg6). **The weakest load-bearing grades the call leans on are ClaimReg9 (`[U]`, party-held — the saving), ClaimReg12 and ClaimReg13 (`[U]`, operator-asserted — the headcount and the twenty miles), and ClaimReg7 (`[V3]`, credible sources disagree on whether attendance improves outcomes).** The call rests on facts I do not have and the board does. This is a statement about evidence quality, not a scored probability.

**Action contingency — what must hold true after acting, which is a separate matter from what would flip the evidence.** The consultation must be capable of changing the outcome, or it is a `[V2]`-shaped process wrapped around a decision already taken, and it will be recognised as such. The charity must have four to six weeks of liquidity to run it. The eastern users' alternative provision must actually accept them, rather than existing on a map. And staff at the named site must be retained through the transition, or Link8 and LoopR2 will make the closure worse than modelled at both sites.

### (c) The dominant unknown

**The twelve-month net saving of each closure, after redundancy, lease exit, dilapidations and lost commissioned income** (ClaimReg9). It is the single fact that would most change this analysis, and the board holds it.

### (d) What to verify first

**Read the governing document and the restricted-funds and title position** (ClaimReg10). It costs an hour of in-house time and nothing in fees, it can be done before Friday, and it can make the entire comparison moot. Then, in order: the staff headcount at risk across both sites against the amended s.188 trigger (ClaimReg4); then the per-site saving model (ClaimReg9).

### (e) What would flip the call

- I am treating the two closures as saving materially similar amounts. **If the eastern closure saves far more and the charity cannot survive otherwise, the call reverses** to the western centre, and the recommendation becomes a managed transfer of western users rather than a closure choice.
- I am treating the charity as having four to six weeks of liquidity. **If it does not, the consultation recommendation is unaffordable**, and the call becomes an emergency closure with a s.188-compliant process run in parallel and the reasons documented contemporaneously.
- I am treating "no alternative within twenty miles" as a real access barrier. **If community transport or a neighbouring provider makes the journey routine, the severity argument for the western site largely collapses** and the reach argument wins on its own terms.
- I am treating both sites as unrestricted. **If either is held on permanent endowment, or the objects name a district, the legal position overrides this analysis entirely.**
- I am treating the closure as unannounced. **If it has already been briefed externally, TipCond4 has fired**, the formative-stage test is failed, and the call becomes damage limitation rather than process design.
- The Step 7 resilience check found the call does not survive Assump1 and Assump2 moving together: if the savings differ sharply *and* there is no time to consult, the choice collapses to whichever closure saves most and the value question is moot.

### (f) What this call rests on

**This call rests on one recorded gate position and five refused questions.** The position: GateQ0, "adviser with no stake" — which is why the brief is written to be checkable by someone who does have a stake, rather than persuasive to someone who does not. The five defaults are named verbatim in the ledger at section 1. **Answering any of them voids the call and the brief is re-derived** — GateQ2 in particular, because the call's stated value trade runs against the operator's own scoping answer.

## 5. The assumption-resilience check

Assumptions the call most rests on, varied together. This is an assumption-fragility test, not system modelling.

- **Assump1** — the two closures produce materially similar savings.
- **Assump2** — the charity is not within weeks of insolvency.
- **Assump3** — the western catchment genuinely has no reachable alternative.
- **Assump4** — neither site is subject to a restricted fund, endowment, or geographically-defined object.

| Varied together | Conclusion survives? |
|---|---|

## 9. Plain-language legend

How to read this: [V] = verified, independently corroborated; [U] = unverified, meaning not independently verified. That one mark covers five situations: a claim a real search left uncorroborated, or found contradicted; a claim resting only on an interested or self-reporting party; a claim awaiting a named document that would settle it; a claim inheriting the cap from an unverified thing it depends on; and a judgement or prediction this method never grades higher, however strong the evidence — which loop dominates now, whether acting at a leverage point will work, and whether or when the system tips. The first three can move if someone looks harder. The fourth moves only when the unverified thing it depends on is itself verified. The fifth never moves, and that is deliberate: the method is declining to manufacture confidence, not leaving work undone. The claim's own line says which of the five it is, and records any contradiction found. Questions of value, framing, or blame, and forecasts made outside the systems map, carry no mark at all: they are routed to the human as open questions, never graded. Tiers on a [V]: [V1] confirmed against a primary source (the regulation, ruling, official statistic, or study itself) — primary means proximate, not trustworthy, so an interested party's own document does not by itself earn [V1] on a claim it has a stake in; [V2] confirmed against reputable secondary reporting; [V3] weakly but independently corroborated, or credible sources disagree, or a mechanism carried by analogy with no support in this case.
