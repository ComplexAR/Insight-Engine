# Closing one of two day centres — Insight Engine analysis

**Problem statement.** A charity in England and Wales must close one of two day centres because a grant has ended and there is a funding shortfall. The eastern centre serves more people; the western centre serves people with no alternative within twenty miles. The board wants a recommendation on Friday. The owner is the board; the operator is an adviser with no stake. The board's stated priority is the financial survival of the charity.

**Step 1 — proportionality triage: full method.** The decision is irreversible in practice, binds service users and family carers who are not in the room, involves material dependency, and turns on contested values. No down-scoping.

**Step 2 — scoping.** Four of five questions answered. Party-held material was not supplied; every figure about the two centres is therefore unverified (see Find7).

- **GateQ1 — the distributive rule.** Closing the eastern centre takes a service from more people, most of whom have some alternative. Closing the western centre takes the only service within twenty miles from fewer people. These two losses cannot be added together. Which rule should the board use: total people served, severity of the worst-off, or money decides and the distribution follows? *(This is the one the call actually turns on.)*
- **GateQ2 — legitimacy.** The people bound by this decision are not in the room. Do users and carers get a say before the choice is made, or are they told afterwards? *(Example position: "we consult before choosing, even if it costs us three weeks.")*
- **GateQ3 — the framing.** You told me one of the two must close. The Court of Appeal held seven weeks ago, on closely analogous facts, that an equivalent binary framing was not forced (Find8). Should the analysis accept the binary, or test it? *(Example position: "accept it — the money is not there and partial operation is fantasy.")*
- **GateQ4 — the systems reading.** Which structure do you believe is currently driving this: managed decline (each cut making the next one necessary), self-fulfilling expectations (the announcement causing the decline that justifies it), or a statutory backstop that will absorb displaced need? This is empirical, not evaluative — evidence cannot settle it *yet*, and it is revisable if evidence arrives.
- **GateQ5 — intervention level.** Do we intervene at this closure, or at the funding model that produced the choice?

## 2. Verified findings

**Find1 — A directly analogous closure decision was quashed seven weeks ago for failure to consult. [V] [V1] — survived disconfirmation.**
The Court of Appeal held that a local authority was under a common law duty to consult a small, readily identifiable group of disabled service users and their carers before deciding to close one of its two centres, and quashed the decision. The court described the group as "99 LLDD individuals regularly using the Poverest Centre and their carers" and held it "would be irrational to close the Poverest Centre without giving them the opportunity to make representations" (at [41]). It recorded that "closure of day centres is undoubtedly a serious step to take from the perspective of users and those who care for them" (at [40], citing earlier authority). Disposal at [58]: the decision is quashed and a fresh consultation must be run "in accordance with Gunning principles".
*Source:* R (BUJ) v London Borough of Bromley [2026] EWCA Civ 888, https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewca/civ/2026/888 — identity-checked against the retrieved document: neutral citation [2026] EWCA Civ 888, Case No CA-2026-000479, Court of Appeal (Civil Division), 10/07/2026, Dingemans LJ (Baker and Whipple LJJ agreeing), on appeal from [2026] EWHC 102 (Admin).
*Disconfirmation:* a search at the negation found the council's argument that an additional showing of prejudice is required before a duty to consult arises, resting on R (Save Stonehenge World Heritage Site Ltd) v SST [2024] EWCA Civ 1227 at [75]; the court rejected it at [42]–[44]. The line of authority spans at least three independent decisions (ex parte Baker, R (LH), and BUJ), so the corroboration does not trace to a single root.
*Party adverse to the finding:* the London Borough of Bromley contested it and lost.

**Find2 — Whether this charity itself owes that duty is not settled. [U] — situation 3: awaiting a named document.**
Find1 binds a local authority. A charity is not a public authority merely because it is funded or contracted by one: in YL v Birmingham City Council [2007] UKHL 27 the House of Lords held that a private care home providing publicly arranged care was not exercising a function of a public nature (Parliament later reversed this for care homes specifically, by amendment to the Health and Social Care Act 2008). Whether this charity is exercising public functions turns on the terms of its commissioning arrangement, which was not supplied. The document that would settle it is the current contract or service-level agreement with the commissioning body, if one exists.
*Source:* https://publications.parliament.uk/pa/ld200607/ldjudgmt/jd070620/birm-3.htm and https://publiclawproject.org.uk/content/uploads/data/resources/63/Judicial-review-and-the-voluntary-sector.pdf — the House of Lords judgment was not opened, so the YL proposition is carried at [V2] and the application to this charity at [U].

**Find3 — The equality-duty argument is the weaker one; the consultation argument is the one that bites. [V] [V1].**
Section 149 of the Equality Act 2010 requires a public authority to have "due regard" to the need to eliminate discrimination and advance equality of opportunity; disability is a relevant protected characteristic. The duty must be exercised "in substance, with rigour and with an open mind" and is non-delegable (R (Bracking) v SSWP [2013] EWCA Civ 1345 at [26], as recorded in BUJ at [47] — the Bracking judgment was not opened, so that citation is carried at [V2]). Where a public authority contracts a service out, the duty stays with the authority. Crucially, in BUJ the equality-duty challenge **failed**: an equality impact assessment that engaged with "the risk of longer travel distances, and the disruption that might bring to users and their families" was enough to discharge it (at [48]–[49]). The board's instinct will be to fear an equality challenge; the decision was quashed on consultation, not on equality.
*Source:* [2026] EWCA Civ 888 at [45]–[49], https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewca/civ/2026/888.

**Find4 — Closing a centre does not extinguish the need; it moves the legal duty. [V] [V2].**
Under section 18 of the Care Act 2014 a local authority must meet the eligible assessed needs of an adult ordinarily resident in its area. Sections 48 to 52 impose a temporary duty on the authority to meet needs where a registered provider becomes unable to carry on because of business failure, regardless of who was funding the care. So the users' need survives the closure and lands, in law, on the authority rather than on the charity.
*Source:* https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2014/23/section/18 and https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2014/23/part/1/crossheading/provider-failure — the primary statutory text was not opened in this session (a retrieval attempt timed out), so this is graded [V2] on the strength of the legislation.gov.uk listings and SCIE's summary at https://www.scie.org.uk/assessment-and-eligibility/key-duties/, not [V1].

**Find5 — The saving is partly a transfer, not an elimination. [V] [V2] — survived disconfirmation, with a named weakness.**
When day centres closed during the pandemic, the withdrawal of structured social contact was harmful to attenders and their family carers, people living with dementia experienced functional decline, and ADASS reported that higher levels of help were sought from other social care services. Sector commentary is explicit that the saving often does not materialise: the British Institute for Learning Disabilities is quoted to the effect that you do not save money if you are serious about supporting people during the day, and a local consultation record questions whether a projected £400,000 saving would in fact be realised.
*Source:* https://arc-sl.nihr.ac.uk/day-centre-resources-hub/research-evidence-day-centres-older-people; https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7197165/; https://www.communitycare.co.uk/content/news/learning-disabilities-are-day-centres-closing-to-cut-costs
*Disconfirmation:* a search at the negation — that closures deliver clean savings — returned no supporting evidence and instead returned a scoping review finding insufficient evidence on which closure policies both minimise cost and improve outcomes (https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/2023/8675499). Corroboration spans academic and sector roots, so it is survived rather than unopposed.
*Named weakness:* the strongest evidence comes from temporary pandemic closures, where users expected to return. Permanent planned closure with a named alternative site is a different mechanism, and the transfer effect may be smaller.

**Find6 — A Friday decision cannot lawfully produce dismissals for at least thirty days, if the redundancy threshold is met. [V] [V2].**
Section 188 of the Trade Union and Labour Relations (Consolidation) Act 1992 requires collective consultation where an employer proposes to dismiss twenty or more employees at one establishment within ninety days; consultation must begin at least thirty days before the first dismissal (forty-five days where one hundred or more are proposed), and the Insolvency Service must receive form HR1 in the same period. The board therefore has more calendar time than "a recommendation on Friday" implies — if the threshold is met, a decision taken on Friday cannot produce a closure until well into October at the earliest.
*Source:* https://uk.practicallaw.thomsonreuters.com/6-200-4673 and https://www.davidsonmorris.com/collective-consultation/ — the statutory text was not opened, so [V2] not [V1]. Whether the twenty-employee threshold is met at either centre is party-held and unknown.

**Find7 — Every fact that would actually decide this is party-held and unverified. [U] — situation 3: awaiting named documents.**
None of the following was supplied, and none can be verified externally: how many more people the eastern centre serves and on what denominator (registered users, attendance-days, or dependency-weighted places); whether "twenty miles" is road distance or straight-line, and whether "no alternative" means no alternative of any kind or no alternative of one specific type; the running cost and net cash saving of each closure after redundancy, dilapidations and exit costs; the tenure of each site and whether either has capital or development value; and whether either building or its funding carries restricted-fund conditions or grant clawback that a closure would trigger. The last of these can reverse the financial answer on its own and is the item most often missed.

**Find8 — The binary framing is itself the most questionable element in what I was given. [V] [V1] on the authority; [U] on its application here.**
On analogous facts the Court of Appeal said in terms: "it is not possible to say whether those properties might not have been considered as suitable for sale, so that it did not have to be a binary choice between the Kentwood Centre and the Poverest Centre" (at [56]). The court also noted the decision had been shaped by the value of the site for affordable housing, and that intermediate options — "some limited provision from the Poverest Centre (or a remodelled part of it)" — remained live. The operator has instructed that the binary is fixed here (GateQ3, deferred), so the analysis accepts it; but the instruction is a scope choice, not an established fact, and it is where the largest hidden issue in this problem sits.
*Source:* [2026] EWCA Civ 888 at [56], https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewca/civ/2026/888.

**Find9 — Part of this is a power problem, not an analysis problem. [U] — situation 5 read across from situation 1: a structural inference no search corroborated.**
The party whose decision created this choice — the funder whose grant ended — bears none of its cost, is not in the room, and has no need to grant any accommodation the charity might seek. Framing the question as "which of our two centres do we close" locates the entire loss inside the charity and its users and leaves the funder's withdrawal unexamined. Any option premised on the funder relenting should be downgraded accordingly. This is an inference from the structure of the case as presented, not a verified claim about any actual funder.

**Over-claims set aside, and why.** Two claims that will be made in the room do not survive: first, that closing the western centre will cause deaths or severe harm — the evidence at Find5 supports functional decline and carer strain, not that, and the stronger claim discredits the weaker one that is actually supportable. Second, that the eastern centre is "more efficient" because it serves more people — headcount is a gameable proxy; a centre can serve more people less often and at lower dependency, and the relevant denominators (attendance-days, dependency-weighted places, cost per occupied place) were not supplied.

**Materially affected parties, including those not in the room.** Users of both centres; family and unpaid carers, who absorb the displaced load first; staff at both centres; whoever holds the statutory duty for the users' assessed needs; other local providers, whose capacity and pricing will be affected; future users who will never present because the service is not there; and the funder whose withdrawal created the choice. Of these, only the board and the operator were represented in any source this analysis was built from.

The *commissioning local authority* is presupposed by my own analysis, not by the case — the operator said the driver was a grant ending, which does not establish any contract with an authority. **Feedback loops**

- `LoopB1` **statutory backstop**: `Link7, Link11` — carer load produces statutory demand, which produces replacement provision, which relieves carer load. This is the loop that decides whether closure is merely costly or genuinely catastrophic. *Exists* **[U]** (the duty-holder node is an introduced entity; the underlying Care Act mechanism is [V2] at Find4); *dominates-now* **[U]**.

*Loops closed but not drawn (the eight-variable cap binds; ungraded self-report).* A **legal-challenge loop** — deciding without consulting invites a challenge, a quashed decision means both centres stay funded longer, reserves fall further, pressure to decide fast rises — needs two variables beyond the cap. A **community loop** — the closure announcement depresses local fundraising and volunteering at the closing site, which lowers reserves — needs one more. Different runs of this case would drop different loops; this list describes one run's choice, not the map's boundary.

**Tipping conditions** — named to watch, not to predict.

- `TipCond1` **[U]** — reserves fall below the level at which the trustees can no longer state a going concern. The decision then passes from the board to insolvency practitioners. *Irreversible.*
- `TipCond2` **[U]** — the funder or commissioner re-tenders the whole service rather than accept a one-centre offer, and the charity loses both. *Hard to reverse.*
- `TipCond3` **[U]** — take-up at the surviving centre falls below its own break-even, and the second closure becomes arithmetic rather than choice. *Hard to reverse.*
- `TipCond4` **[U]** — a consultation challenge succeeds and the decision is quashed, forcing a re-run while both centres are still funded. *Reversible, at cost.*

**Emergence.** Once a closure is announced or leaks, users disengage, staff apply elsewhere and funders discount the charity — all before anything closes. The resulting decline is then cited as evidence that the closure was necessary. The shared expectation is itself a causal variable, and it is the only element here that acts faster than the board does. **[U]**

1. `LevPoint1` **the choice set itself.** *Mechanism* **[V1]** — BUJ at [56]: an equivalent binary "did not have to be a binary choice". *Effectiveness* **[U]**.
2. `LevPoint2` **the sequence** — consult before choosing rather than after. *Mechanism* **[V1]** (BUJ at [41], [58]). *Effectiveness* **[U]**.
3. `LevPoint3` **the duty holder** — putting the displaced need in writing to whoever carries the Care Act duty, before deciding. *Mechanism* **[V2]** (Find4). *Effectiveness* **[U]**.
4. `LevPoint4` **the delay in Link3 and Link7** — surfacing the lagged displaced cost before the vote rather than discovering it after. *Mechanism* **[V3]**. *Effectiveness* **[U]**.
5. `LevPoint5` **the announcement** — how and when it is made, because of the emergence above. *Mechanism* **[U]**. *Effectiveness* **[U]**.

**The missing driver (omission check).** The most plausible driver not in this map is **the property**. If one site is freehold with sale or development value and the other is leased, capital value rather than service value is probably the operative cause, and no arrow drawn above would detect it. In the analogous case, the site's suitability for affordable housing was central to the decision.

**Boundary disclosure.** This map adopts the board's framing: one of two must close, financial survival first. Users of both centres, family carers, staff, and the statutory duty holder are materially affected and absent from every source it was built from. Placed outside the boundary: merger, transfer of one centre to another provider, reduced-hours operation of both, and closing the charity entirely. That exclusion is a **value judgement about scope, distinct from [U] evidential uncertainty**. Under the western users' framing the map would centre on irreversibility rather than on reserves, and Node3 would be the primary variable rather than a driver of Node2 — which is why the twenty-mile figure appears in what-to-verify-first below.

## 4. Assumption-resilience check

The call rests most heavily on four assumptions, varied together rather than one at a time.

- `Assump1` — the charity has at least thirty days of cash runway.
- `Assump2` — whoever holds the statutory duty will engage within that window.
- `Assump3` — the net cash saving of each closure is not already known to the finance lead.
- `Assump4` — the charity is not itself exercising a public function (Find2).

**Result.** Moving `Assump1` and `Assump2` to false **together** breaks the call: with no runway and no counterparty, consulting first is unaffordable and the recommendation collapses to an immediate decision plus an orderly conversation with the Charity Commission. Moving `Assump3` to false shortens the call rather than breaking it — if the figures exist, the board can decide in days rather than weeks. Moving `Assump4` to false **hardens** the call: consultation stops being prudence and becomes a legal requirement. Moving `Assump1` alone, or `Assump2` alone, leaves the call standing.

**Coverage.** These four were varied together. Two load-bearing assumptions were **not** varied: that closing one centre is genuinely necessary (that is GateQ3, deferred), and that the board's Friday vote is the operative decision rather than one step in a longer process. What fraction of the assumption space this covers is unknowable and is not claimed.

## 5. The decision brief

### (a) The call — a provisional stance, not a verdict

This is a wicked problem: the deep core dominates, the decisive facts are party-held, and the operator deferred the value question the choice turns on. The call is therefore **explicitly re-openable**.

**Do not name a centre on Friday. Resolve on Friday to close one centre in principle, and to make the choice between them after a consultation with users and carers of both.** Specifically, the Friday resolution should commit to three things: a Gunning-compliant consultation with users and carers of both centres; a written request to whoever holds the statutory duty for these users, setting out the displaced need under each option and asking what they will commission; and production of the four party-held figures at (d) below.

**This prioritises the defensibility and the financial accuracy of the decision at the expense of speed**, and it accepts three to six weeks of continued dual running to get them.

**If the board insists on naming a centre on Friday**, the analysis cannot name one on the operator's own stated priority, because which closure saves more money is unknown (Find7). Forced to choose blind, the western centre is the wrong one to close: the twenty-mile gap is the only asset in this case that cannot be substituted, whereas headcount can be served, partially, from elsewhere. **That tiebreak comes from the default I embedded for the deferred GateQ1, not from the board** — see (b).

**Re-openers, as a trigger list for `track`** (each: indicator → what it would change):

- The finance lead's net-saving figures for both options → resolves the dominant unknown; may reverse which closure is affordable.
- A restricted-fund or grant-clawback condition found on either building → may reverse the financial answer outright.
- Freehold or development value found on either site → surfaces the missing driver; may show the real decision was never about service.
- A written response from the statutory duty holder → determines whether `LoopB1` operates.
- Cash runway falling below thirty days → triggers `TipCond1`; the primary call fails.
- A user or carer indicating an intention to challenge → moves Find1 from prudence to exposure.
- The operator answering any deferred gate question → **voids the call**; the brief is re-derived.

### (b) Confidence basis, and the action contingency

**Confidence in the fact-chain is asymmetric.** The legal exposure is strong: Find1 is [V1], identity-checked against the judgment, decided seven weeks ago, on facts that match this case unusually closely — two centres, one closing, disabled users, longer journeys. Everything about *these two centres* is [U] (Find7), as is whether the duty in Find1 reaches this charity at all (Find2). **The weakest load-bearing grades the call leans on are Find7 [U] and Find2 [U]**; Find4 and Find6 are [V2] because the primary texts were not opened in this session. This is a statement about evidence quality, not a scored probability.

**The call also rests on a recorded gate position and four embedded defaults.** GateQ0 records the operator as an adviser with no stake, so nothing here is discounted for interest. GateQ1 was deferred, and in its place the call embeds: *financial survival governs; where the money is unknown or equal, fall back to irreversibility*. That default, not the board, is what produces the western-centre tiebreak in the contingent branch above. **Revising GateQ1 voids the call.**

**Action contingency — what must hold true *after* acting, which is a different thing from what would flip the evidence at (e).** For the primary call to succeed: the charity must be able to fund three to six weeks of dual running; the consultation must reach users who may not read letters, which for this population means carers, staff and the centres themselves rather than a website; and the duty holder must answer within the window. Every premise at (a) could be correct and the call still fail on any of these three.

### (c) The dominant unknown

**The net cash effect of each closure — not the user numbers.** Which of the two options actually leaves the charity solvent, after redundancy costs, dilapidations, exit costs, property tenure and any restricted-fund or grant clawback. The board is currently framing the decision on the one dimension it can see (how many people each centre serves) and deciding on a dimension it has not measured (which closure saves the charity).

### (d) What to verify first

**One page from the finance lead, for each centre: net annual cash saving after redundancy and exit costs; tenure and any capital or development value; and any restricted-fund or grant condition triggered by closure.** This costs hours, not weeks, is entirely within the charity's own records, and can reverse the answer. Second, and nearly as cheap: establish whether "twenty miles" is road distance to a comparable service or straight-line to anything, and whether the twenty-employee threshold at Find6 is met — the latter tells the board how much calendar time it actually has.

### (e) What would flip the call

- I am treating the charity as having at least thirty days of cash runway. **If it does not**, the primary call reverses: decide immediately and open an orderly conversation with the Charity Commission and the funder.
- I am treating the eastern centre as larger by headcount but not necessarily more expensive. **If the western centre costs more per attendance-day and reserves are near the floor**, the financial branch and the distributive branch conflict, and the deferred GateQ1 becomes decisive rather than a tiebreak.
- I am treating "twenty miles" as road distance to a comparable service. **If it is straight-line, or "no alternative" means no alternative of one narrow type**, the irreversibility asymmetry that carries the contingent tiebreak weakens.
- I am treating this charity as not exercising a public function. **If the commissioning arrangement makes it one, or if a local authority's own decommissioning decision is in play**, consultation stops being prudent and becomes mandatory, and the primary call hardens from advice into requirement.
- **Jointly (from the resilience check):** if the funding cliff is immediate **and** the duty holder will not engage **and** the two options are financially equivalent, no branch survives. The honest position then is that the board is choosing between two harms with no information — a fact to minute as such, not an analysis to rely on.
- **Tipping conditions:** `TipCond1` (going-concern floor), `TipCond2` (whole-service re-tender), `TipCond3` (surviving centre below break-even), `TipCond4` (decision quashed) each change the decision the board is actually taking, and are named to watch rather than to predict.

## 7. Plain-language legend

*How to read this: [V] = verified, independently corroborated; [U] = unverified, meaning not independently verified. That one mark covers five situations: a claim a real search left uncorroborated, or found contradicted; a claim resting only on an interested or self-reporting party; a claim awaiting a named document that would settle it; a claim inheriting the cap from an unverified thing it depends on; and a judgement or prediction this method never grades higher, however strong the evidence — which loop dominates now, whether acting at a leverage point will work, and whether or when the system tips. The first three can move if someone looks harder. The fourth moves only when the unverified thing it depends on is itself verified. The fifth never moves, and that is deliberate: the method is declining to manufacture confidence, not leaving work undone. The claim's own line says which of the five it is, and records any contradiction found. Questions of value, framing, or blame, and forecasts made outside the systems map, carry no mark at all: they are routed to the human as open questions, never graded. Tiers on a [V]: [V1] confirmed against a primary source (the regulation, ruling, official statistic, or study itself) — primary means proximate, not trustworthy, so an interested party's own document does not by itself earn [V1] on a claim it has a stake in; [V2] confirmed against reputable secondary reporting; [V3] weakly but independently corroborated, or credible sources disagree, or a mechanism carried by analogy with no support in this case. Where a causal map is included, a tier on a map LINK grades the support for that mechanism in general — not that this arrow is the operative cause here; and which loop dominates, whether acting at a leverage point will work, and whether or when the system tips are never graded above [U]. "Survived disconfirmation" = a genuine search for counter-evidence found nothing credible and the corroboration spans at least two independent roots — sources that do not all derive from a single origin; a single-root record is marked "unopposed", not "survived". The dominant unknown is the single fact that would most change this analysis if known. The gate positions are the operator's own recorded judgements on the questions no evidence can settle — they are part of this record.*
