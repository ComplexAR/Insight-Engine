### Findings

`Find1` — **The comparison you have been given is the wrong one.** "Serves more people" measures total benefit. Closure destroys *marginal* benefit: the difference the service makes against each person's next best alternative. On your own description that difference is near-total in the west and partial in the east. This does not by itself decide the case, because the marginal benefit of the eastern centre could still exceed the western one in aggregate — but the board has not been shown the number that would tell it. Grade `[U]` as applied — inherits the cap from `PartyHeld1` and `PartyHeld2`.

`Find2` — **"Twenty miles" is a statement about buildings, not about people.** It is the most confidently-asserted number in the framing and it is doing the work of a needs assessment without being one. What matters is journey time by transport actually available to a frail person, and how many people would be left with nothing at all. Twenty miles along a bus route is a different fact from eight miles with no bus. Grade `[U]` — awaiting `PartyHeld2`.

`Find3` — **The binary removes the options most likely to beat it.** Absent from the question as posed: reduced opening days at both sites, shared or cheaper premises in one catchment, a transport subsidy instead of a building, transfer of one service to another provider, merger, and closing neither while cutting elsewhere. Your `GateQ2` position adopts this finding. Grade `[U]` — structural inference.

`Find4` — **The money may not be fungible, and the buildings may not be yours to sell.** If the western centre was built from a dedicated appeal, those funds may be restricted to that purpose, and s.117 means disposal of either site is not an act the board takes alone (`ClaimReg5` `[V1]`). A closure that releases no money is not a saving; it is a service withdrawal with the same deficit afterwards. Grade `[U]` as applied — awaiting `PartyHeld4`.

`Find5` — **Your position is a conflict of interest and has to be managed, not hidden or apologised for.** You led the fundraising that built the western centre and your stated starting point favours it. Under `ClaimReg1`, identifying and managing conflicts is one of the seven principles, and the test is not whether you are actually biased but whether the decision could reasonably appear to have been influenced. Adverse finding against a named party: **the party was given the opportunity to respond, and in fact volunteered the disclosure unprompted**, which under this method is weighted as a self-damaging admission and treated accordingly. Not contested. The practical consequence is a minute, a declaration, and a board decision on whether you participate in the vote — not a reason to discount your criterion.

`Find6` — **The timetable is the largest single exposure, and it is self-imposed.** A closure decision taken on Friday without person-level data, a net financial figure, or any consultation is precisely the pattern that `ClaimReg1` principles 3 and 4 are written about. Grade `[V1]` on what the principles require; `[U]` on whether this board would breach them, since I have not seen what the board already holds. Separately, if 20 or more staff are at risk across the closure, `ClaimReg8` `[V2]` puts a 30-day floor under the process regardless of what the board decides on Friday.

`Find7` — **Where the users' rights actually sit.** A board may assume that closing a service engages its own human rights obligations. Under `ClaimReg2` `[V1]` it does not: s.73 reaches personal care at home and residential accommodation, not day services. The duty-holder for people with assessed needs remains the local authority. Two consequences: the charity has more legal freedom here than it may think, and it should not assume the local authority will quietly absorb displaced western users — that is a conversation to have before the decision, not after.

`Find8` — **Power check.** Nothing before me establishes that the outcome is being determined by power rather than merits. But I cannot rule it out, and it is the map's missing driver — see below.

## 4. Decision brief

### (a) The call

**Recommend that Friday's board does not choose between the two centres, and takes four resolutions instead.**

1. Neither centre closes on Friday. The board records the financial gap, its date, and the tests any option must meet.
2. Management produces, within a named and short period: the net financial effect of each option (`PartyHeld3`); a person-level attendance and alternative-provision analysis for both catchments (`PartyHeld1`, `PartyHeld2`); a legal check on whether either site's land or funds are restricted (`PartyHeld4`, against `ClaimReg5`); and an options list that includes reduced days, shared premises, transport, transfer to another provider, and closing neither.
3. A consultation with users, carers and staff at both sites opens, with it made clear that trustees take the final decision.
4. The Chair's interest in the western centre is declared in the minutes and the board decides how it is managed, including whether the Chair votes.

**This is a provisional stance, not a verdict.** The problem is wicked: the deep core dominates and no amount of verification settles which loss counts for more. The stance prioritises the defensibility and correctness of an irreversible decision at the expense of speed, and of the cost of carrying both centres for the additional weeks. It is explicitly re-openable on the conditions in (e).

The stance follows directly from `LevPoint1` and `LevPoint2`, and from your own `GateQ2` and `GateQ3` positions. It does not tell you which centre to close, because on the evidence available nobody can tell you that — including anyone who offers to.

### (b) Confidence basis, and the separate action contingency

**Confidence in the fact chain.** The legal findings are firm: `ClaimReg1`, `ClaimReg2`, `ClaimReg3` and `ClaimReg5` are `[V1]` against primary sources with currency statements, opened and identity-checked. The weakest load-bearing grade the call leans on is `ClaimReg7` at `[V2]`, marked *unopposed — single-root record*, with pandemic-period confounding — it is what supports the cost-migration link, and it is the softest thing here. `ClaimReg8` at `[V2]` was not identity-checked against the statute. This is evidence-quality transparency, not a scored probability.

**Action contingency — what must hold true after acting.** Distinct from (e). For this call to succeed rather than merely be defensible: the deficit must tolerate the delay without the charity trading while insolvent; management must actually be able to produce `PartyHeld3` and `PartyHeld4` in the period set, rather than the deferral becoming drift; and the consultation must be capable of changing the outcome, because a consultation that cannot is worse than none — it adds cost, delay and a documented grievance without adding information.

### (c) The dominant unknown

**Whether closing either centre actually releases money to the charity.** If the western centre's funds are restricted, or its land is not freely disposable under `ClaimReg5`, or lease exit and redundancy costs consume the saving, then both options are wrong and the frame collapses. Every other unknown selects *between* the two options; this one determines whether the choice exists.

Named alongside it, from the boundary disclosure: the analysis has been built entirely from the charity's framing, and the people whose week ends have not been heard.

### (d) What to verify first

**The restricted-funds and land check on the western centre.** It is the cheapest of the four — the appeal literature, the title, and the governing document, which is likely a day's work — and it is the only one that can invalidate the entire question rather than answer it. Do this before the person-level analysis, which is more valuable but takes weeks.

### (e) What would flip the call

Each as a first-person conditional.

- *I am treating the deficit as tolerating a delay of some weeks.* If the charity faces insolvency on a date, the call reverses: trustees cannot consult their way through insolvency, and the recommendation becomes to decide on Friday, record the constraint that forced it, and consult only on implementation and mitigation.
- *I am treating the board as free to defer.* If a funder, landlord or commissioner deadline already binds — the map's missing driver — the call is void, because this is then execution and not choice.
- **Jointly.** The Step 7 resilience check varied these two together, and the call does not survive it: urgent deficit *and* an external deadline together defeat the stance entirely. Varied singly, the call survives both.
- *I am treating closure as effectively irreversible* (`TipCond2`, `TipCond3`). If the western site can be mothballed and the service restarted, the cost of deciding quickly falls and the call weakens substantially — though it does not remove the requirement to be sufficiently informed. Urgency and reversibility together weaken it furthest short of flipping.
- *I am treating the headline saving as gross* — the `GateQ4` default, which is mine and not yours. If management produces a net figure that survives lease, redundancy and restricted-fund effects, that default is discharged and (c) is answered.

**Assumptions I did not vary:** that you are in England or Wales (`Assump1`); that the driver is financial (`Assump3`); and that the two centres provide comparable services. I cannot say what share of the assumption space that leaves untested — that denominator is unknowable.

### The gate positions this call rests on, and the first re-opener

The call depends on `GateQ2` (the binary must be justified first) and `GateQ3` (Friday is a proposal, not a closure). Revising either voids it. It embeds two defaults: `GateQ1` on your criterion, and `GateQ4` on mine.

**First re-opener — my disagreement, argued rather than buried in the default slot.** The `GateQ1` default applies your stated criterion, that the first duty is to those with nowhere else to go. I have embedded it because it is yours and because it is a defensible charitable criterion. I do not think it is wrong. I think it cannot yet be applied, for two reasons the board should hear.

First, "no alternative within twenty miles" describes buildings, not people (`Find2`). Applying your criterion honestly requires knowing how many western attenders genuinely have nothing, and whether any eastern attenders do — and it is possible that the criterion, correctly applied, points east. That fact has not been gathered, because the frame assumed the answer.

Second, the criterion arrived from the person who raised the money that built the western centre (`Find5`). That does not make it wrong and I am not treating it as disqualifying. It does mean the board must adopt the criterion for itself, on the record, before the criterion decides anything — otherwise a defensible principle carries a decision that will later be read as a personal one.

Answering `GateQ1` in the board's own voice, with `PartyHeld2` in front of it, settles this.

## 6. Plain-language legend

How to read this: [V] = verified, independently corroborated; [U] = unverified, meaning not independently verified. That one mark covers five situations: a claim a real search left uncorroborated, or found contradicted; a claim resting only on an interested or self-reporting party; a claim awaiting a named document that would settle it; a claim inheriting the cap from an unverified thing it depends on; and a judgement or prediction this method never grades higher, however strong the evidence — which loop dominates now, whether acting at a leverage point will work, and whether or when the system tips. The first three can move if someone looks harder. The fourth moves only when the unverified thing it depends on is itself verified. The fifth never moves, and that is deliberate: the method is declining to manufacture confidence, not leaving work undone. The claim's own line says which of the five it is, and records any contradiction found. Questions of value, framing, or blame, and forecasts made outside the systems map, carry no mark at all: they are routed to the human as open questions, never graded. Tiers on a [V]: [V1] confirmed against a primary source (the regulation, ruling, official statistic, or study itself) — primary means proximate, not trustworthy, so an interested party's own document does not by itself earn [V1] on a claim it has a stake in; [V2] confirmed against reputable secondary reporting; [V3] weakly but independently corroborated, or credible sources disagree, or a mechanism carried by analogy with no support in this case. Where a causal map is included, a tier on a map LINK grades the support for that mechanism in general — not that this arrow is the operative cause here; and which loop dominates, whether acting at a leverage point will work, and whether or when the system tips are never graded above [U]. "Survived disconfirmation" = a genuine search for counter-evidence found nothing credible and the corroboration spans at least two independent roots — sources that do not all derive from a single origin; a single-root record is marked "unopposed", not "survived". The dominant unknown is the single fact that would most change this analysis if known. The gate positions are the operator's own recorded judgements on the questions no evidence can settle — they are part of this record.
