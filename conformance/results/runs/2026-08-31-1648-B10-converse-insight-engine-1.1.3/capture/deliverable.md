## 2. Findings

**Find1 — The comparison the board has been handed is not decidable on the two facts given. [U], situation 3.**
"Serves more people" and "no alternative within twenty miles" are the only figures in the question, and both are unaudited. A headcount without dependency level, years of attendance, or hours per week is not a measure of harm avoided. A distance is not a journey: twenty miles with a community transport scheme and twenty miles without one are different facts. No figure about either centre was supplied to this analysis, so nothing in the comparison has been verified. *Source: the operator's own framing; scoping was offered and skipped.*

**Find2 — Closure moves cost; it does not remove it. [V] [V2].**
Where users have assessed eligible needs under the Care Act 2014, the local authority's duty to meet those needs survives the closure of any particular provider. The charity's saving and the public cost are two different numbers, and only the first is in the board's paper. *Source: Care Act 2014, Part 1; SCIE, key Care Act duties.*

**Find3 — Travel distance is a documented determinant of whether a day service is used at all. [V] [V2], survived disconfirmation.**
Transport is repeatedly identified as a barrier to social participation among older and disabled people, with car access associated with up to three times higher participation in formal activity, and rural residents facing compounding structural barriers. Day centres are also documented as doing more than their stated purpose — offsetting isolation and providing incidental monitoring of health and wellbeing. A negation search for evidence that closures cause no adverse effect, or that community alternatives outperform building-based services, returned nothing of comparable strength: only closing authorities' own assertions, which are interested-party accounts and are set aside here on that ground. Two independent source roots. *Sources: Orellana et al., BMC Geriatrics 2020; public transportation and social isolation, scoping review; rural ageing-in-place needs assessment.*

**Find4 — A comparable closure was quashed this year, and the reasoning bears directly on both the timetable and the option set. [V] [V1].**
In *R (BUJ) v London Borough of Bromley* [2026] EWCA Civ 888, handed down 10 July 2026, the Court of Appeal (Dingemans LJ, Baker and Whipple LJJ agreeing) quashed a council's decision to close a centre used by 99 learners with learning difficulties and disabilities. Three points carry across. First, a common-law duty to consult arose because the users were a small, readily identifiable group for some of whom the centre was the centre of their lives outside the home. Second, relief was not withheld under s31(2A) Senior Courts Act 1981: that the council might have decided the same way was "possible, perhaps probable", but not "highly likely", which is the statutory threshold. Third, the court observed that the choice had not necessarily been binary as between the two centres — a longer list of properties had been narrowed, and continued limited provision on the site alongside redevelopment had not been tested. Notably, the equality-duty challenge *failed*: the impact assessment had addressed travel distances and disruption adequately. It was the failure to consult, not the failure to assess, that was fatal. *Identity-checked against the primary judgment: caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewca/civ/2026/888 returns a document bearing that neutral citation and naming Dingemans and the Poverest Centre. Secondary reporting: Solicitors Journal; Local Government Lawyer.*

**Find5 — Whether that duty binds this charity is open, and the answer is in a document nobody has read here. [U], situation 3.**
A genuine disconfirmation search found real counter-structure. *YL v Birmingham CC* [2007] UKHL 27 held that a private care home was not exercising a public function. That was reversed for care homes by s145 Health and Social Care Act 2008, which ceased to have effect on 1 April 2015 and was replaced by s73 Care Act 2014 — which reaches registered providers of personal care in the home, or accommodation with nursing or personal care, and on its face may not reach a day centre. Separately, s149(2) Equality Act 2010 applies the public sector equality duty to non-public authorities when they exercise public functions [V] [V2]. The framework is settled; its application to this charity is not, and turns on the commissioning contract.

**Find6 — There is no general legal duty on charity trustees to consult beneficiaries before closing a service. [V] [V3].** Weakness recorded: *inferred from the absence of any such duty in the guidance, not from a source stating the negative.* The gap between what the law requires of the charity and what it required of the council in Find4 is the space in which this decision sits, and the board should know it is choosing, not complying. *Source: NCVO, closing your charity.*

**Find7 — The trustees' duty runs to the charity's purposes, not to either site. [V] [V1].**
Acting in the charity's best interests means doing what best enables it to carry out its purposes, now and in future. Closing a service, merging, or bringing the charity to a close can all be consistent with that duty. This cuts both ways: it licenses closure, and it forbids treating either centre's constituency as owed something the governing document does not owe them. *Source: Charity Commission, CC3 The essential trustee.*

**Find8 — If the charity is near insolvency, this is a different decision. [V] [V2].**
Trustees can be personally liable for wrongful trading. A charity need not stop the moment it is insolvent, but it may continue only where the trustees reach and record a considered view that insolvent liquidation is realistically avoidable, and must not worsen the position of creditors. Whatever is decided on Friday, the reasoning must be minuted. *Sources: ACEVO / CFG, Charities and insolvency (March 2020); Moore Kingston Smith.*

**Find9 — The redundancy timetable may already constrain Friday. [V] [V2].**
Twenty or more proposed redundancies at one establishment within 90 days triggers collective consultation under s188 TULRCA 1992: a minimum of 30 days for 20 to 99, 45 days for 100 or more, with form HR1 to the Insolvency Service. If either site crosses the threshold, the earliest possible closure date is already fixed by statute and is not a matter of board preference. *Sources: Weightmans; CM Murray.*

**Find10 — Recovered on the coverage re-scan; three items the focus above had pushed aside. [U], situation 3.**
Restricted funds and area of benefit: if either site was built or funded by restricted donations, a legacy, or a grant tied to a locality, the board may not be free to close it, or may face a repayment. Older charities frequently have a geographic area of benefit in their objects, which would constrain the choice legally rather than ethically. TUPE: if the service transfers to another provider instead of closing, the employment position and the cost model both change materially, which alters the comparison between the two options. Safeguarding: transition between services is a recognised point of elevated risk for vulnerable adults, and the duty sits with the charity throughout the transition, not only up to the closing date.

**Setting aside.** Nothing in the material supplied attributes fault to any named person, and none is introduced here. No finding in this brief is adverse to a named party, so no opportunity-to-respond record arises. One claim was tested and abandoned: that closure savings are systematically overstated because central overheads do not scale down and stranded costs land up front. A real search found nothing of comparable strength in this domain, so it is graded [U], situation 1, and nothing rests on it. It remains a reasonable question for the finance paper, not a finding.

**Is this a power problem?** On the evidence supplied, no — no party is in a position to impose its preference regardless of merits. But that assessment is only as good as the input: if a funder or commissioner has in fact already determined which site closes, and the board's paper is a ratification exercise, that changes the character of the whole decision, and this brief would need re-running on that footing.

## 3. The decision brief

### (a) The call — a provisional stance, not a verdict

This is a wicked problem: the deep core dominates it, every charity-specific fact is [U], and there is no verifiable solution to be found. What follows is a stance, and it is explicitly re-openable.

**On your recorded positions, the defensible Friday resolution is not "close the eastern centre". It is a two-part resolution: an in-principle direction that the western service is retained in some form, and an instruction that no closure is executed until the option set has been tested and the affected users consulted.**

The direction follows from GateQ1: you have recorded that severity of deprivation outweighs headcount, and the western users are the ones for whom no substitute exists within reach. The condition follows from GateQ2 and GateQ3 together: you have recorded that the decision should take effect only after consultation, and that the board has not tested whether the binary is real. A board that resolves to close a specific site on Friday, having never appraised anything but two options, is deciding on an untested premise — and Find4 shows that is precisely where a comparable decision failed.

**This prioritises the irreplaceability of the western service at the expense of the greater number served in the east, and it accepts a delay cost to do so.** That is the trade, stated plainly. It is your trade, taken at GateQ1, not mine.

Per GateQ5, the board should commission the structural review alongside, with a reporting date in the minute. Closing a building without changing the model postpones this decision rather than making it.

### (b) Confidence basis, and separately, what must hold after acting

The legal and evidential spine is reasonably firm: Find4 is [V1] against the primary judgment, Find3 is [V2] and survived disconfirmation, Find7 is [V1].

The weakest load-bearing grades the call leans on are two, both [U]: **Find1**, that every charity-specific figure is unverified party-held material, and **Find5**, that whether public-law consultation duties bind this charity at all is unresolved. The consultation element of the call therefore rests on your GateQ2 position and on prudence, not on a verified legal obligation — Find6 records that no general duty binds you. This is evidence-quality transparency; it is not a scored probability and should not be read as one.

**Action contingency — what must hold true after acting, distinct from what would flip the evidence.** First, the local authority must in fact absorb or re-provide for the displaced eastern users; if it does not, the harm the call accepts is larger than the call assumes. Second, the interval between Friday's in-principle decision and its execution must not itself hollow out the site named for closure. The moment an intention becomes public, attendance at the named site can fall for reasons unconnected to demand, after which the figures appear to justify what was already decided. Controlled, simultaneous communication to both sites is a condition of the call working, not a presentational detail.

### (c) The dominant unknown

**The terms and portability of the funding attached to each site.** It determines two things at once: whether closing a given site produces the saving claimed, and whether any public-law duty attaches to the charity at all. Nothing else in this analysis would move as much.

### (d) What to verify first

Two documents, both obtainable before Friday, either of which can invert the decision outright:

1. **The commissioning contract or grant agreement for each site** — termination terms, minimum-numbers clauses, clawback, and whether the charity is thereby exercising a public function (Find5).
2. **The governing document** — area of benefit, and any restricted fund or legacy tied to either site (Find10).

Then, in the following week: the lease and dilapidations position for each building; attendance by individual with dependency level and years attending; costs separated into those that actually cease on closure and those that do not; and staff numbers per site against the s188 threshold (Find9).

**To monitor,** three conditions rather than dates or probabilities. If attendance at the retained site falls below any minimum-numbers or block-funding basis in the contract, the funding model breaks and is hard to restore. If a displaced user's needs escalate to crisis or residential care, that is irreversible for that person. If reserves approach the point where trustees can no longer record a realistic prospect of avoiding insolvent liquidation, the question stops being which centre.

### (e) What would flip the call

Each is written as a condition I am currently treating as true.

- I am treating the twenty-mile figure as meaning no practical access. **If a community transport scheme, a partner provider, or a satellite arrangement covers that area, the irreplaceability claim collapses and the call reverses.**
- I am treating the eastern users as having genuine alternatives. **If those alternatives are nominal — full, unsuitable, or equally distant — the distributive contrast disappears, GateQ1 stops discriminating between the sites, and the decision reverts to cost.**
- I am treating either closure as producing the saving claimed. **If a restricted fund, a clawback clause, or a dilapidations liability attaches to the western site, closing the eastern one may be the only solvent option and the call reverses (Find10).**
- Jointly, from the resilience check: I am treating the funding gap as real *and* Friday as a genuine constraint. **If both are soft, the decision is premature and should not be taken this week at all** — those two assumptions moved together are the pair the call cannot survive.
- From the systems pass: **if reserves are already near the point at which continued trading cannot be justified, the call is void** — the question becomes whether the charity may continue at all (Find8), not which centre closes.
- From the deferred GateQ4: **if it emerges that anticipatory withdrawal is already running, the attendance comparison is partly an artefact of the board's own signalling** and any figure gathered after the intention became known must be discounted before it is relied on.

**Coverage of the resilience check, stated plainly.** Four assumptions were varied together: that the charity cannot fund both, that eastern users have alternatives, that western users have none, and that Friday is fixed. Two further load-bearing assumptions were not tested — that the charity survives either closure, and that the local authority will in fact meet displaced eligible needs. What fraction of the assumption space this covers is unknowable, and no fraction is implied.

## Plain-language legend

How to read this: [V] = verified, independently corroborated; [U] = unverified, meaning not independently verified. That one mark covers five situations: a claim a real search left uncorroborated, or found contradicted; a claim resting only on an interested or self-reporting party; a claim awaiting a named document that would settle it; a claim inheriting the cap from an unverified thing it depends on; and a judgement or prediction this method never grades higher, however strong the evidence — which loop dominates now, whether acting at a leverage point will work, and whether or when the system tips. The first three can move if someone looks harder. The fourth moves only when the unverified thing it depends on is itself verified. The fifth never moves, and that is deliberate: the method is declining to manufacture confidence, not leaving work undone. The claim's own line says which of the five it is, and records any contradiction found. Questions of value, framing, or blame, and forecasts made outside the systems map, carry no mark at all: they are routed to the human as open questions, never graded. Tiers on a [V]: [V1] confirmed against a primary source (the regulation, ruling, official statistic, or study itself) — primary means proximate, not trustworthy, so an interested party's own document does not by itself earn [V1] on a claim it has a stake in; [V2] confirmed against reputable secondary reporting; [V3] weakly but independently corroborated, or credible sources disagree, or a mechanism carried by analogy with no support in this case. Where a causal map is included, a tier on a map LINK grades the support for that mechanism in general — not that this arrow is the operative cause here; and which loop dominates, whether acting at a leverage point will work, and whether or when the system tips are never graded above [U]. "Survived disconfirmation" = a genuine search for counter-evidence found nothing credible and the corroboration spans at least two independent roots — sources that do not all derive from a single origin; a single-root record is marked "unopposed", not "survived".
